# Ad Copy Flow

Ad copy should feel like a natural conversation, not a script. It guides the viewer from awareness to resolution through rhythm, timing, and alignment with visuals.

## Conversational Rhythm

The best ad copy doesn't sound rehearsed. It flows like a real person speaking to someone they trust. When the words match the pace of thought, the audience feels included instead of sold to. This rhythm builds trust and makes complex ideas feel simple. The most effective structure starts with a real homeowner's voice, then layers in news context, followed by the hidden mechanism and final resolution, all without a hard sell.

## Copy-B-roll Synchronization

Every line of copy should have a visual partner in the B-roll. The edit shouldn't lag or jump ahead. When the voiceover says "this is where it gets real," the camera cuts to a close-up of solar panels under sunlight. That alignment turns information into experience. Smooth transitions between spoken words and visuals keep attention locked in and prevent disengagement.

## Avoiding Jarring Transitions

If the copy stops mid-sentence while the video keeps moving, or if the B-roll shows something unrelated, the viewer loses focus. The flow breaks. Keep both elements moving in sync so the story feels continuous, not stitched together. This is especially critical when shifting from emotional storytelling to technical explanation.

## Structural Flow: From Problem to Resolution

The winning copy structure follows a clear arc: real homeowner voice → news context → hidden mechanism → resolution → close. This sequence mirrors how people actually process information. It starts with relatable pain, grounds the issue in reality, reveals what's been overlooked, then delivers clarity and solution without pushing. The result is not persuasion but revelation.
