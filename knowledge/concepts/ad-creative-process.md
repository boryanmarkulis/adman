# The Ad Creative Process

A structured creative process is how you consistently beat top-performing ads by turning insight into proof-driven storytelling.

## The Core Principle: Proof Is Everything

The most important part of an ad isn't the hook, the music, or even the visuals. It's **proof**. Without it, your message floats in a vacuum. With it, you anchor attention to real outcomes, user experiences, or measurable results. This is what separates convincing ads from clever noise.

Real customer language from Reddit and client calls is the primary source for effective ad angles in the target state solar campaigns. You don't invent pain points. You find them. the mentor brand ad-creative automation system collects real customer video feedback and uses it to generate both raw ad material and copy data fed to Claude for AI-generated ad copy grounded in authentic language.

Optimizing for verbatim sentence reuse from Reddit creates fragmented copy; instead, weaving 60 to 80 percent of real language into a coherent narrative yields better results. The goal isn't mimicry. It's resonance.

Unverified secondary-source figures must be excluded until confirmed by a human. Even if they appear credible, they don't belong in the creative unless backed by primary sources like official filings or direct customer data.

## The Process: Structure Beats Intuition

You can't rely on inspiration alone to outperform the competition. A repeatable creative process is how you beat top-performing ads. It's not about being more creative in a random way. It's about systematically testing, validating, and refining ideas using real data and user behavior.

This means building a workflow that starts with clear proof points, turns them into variations, tests them at scale, and uses the results to feed the next round of creation. The process itself becomes the lever.

Three evidence streams converge to create a repeatable production system: brand performance signals, industry social scrapes, and expert course playbooks. These inputs shape one brief, forming the foundation for every creative test.

The process is validated through real-world testing. For example, the "bigger bill" version of an ad was tested against July start digit and wince trigger variations, each designed to trigger self-identification without exclusion. The "bigger bill" version proved most effective because it aligns with a real sales logic: larger bills support larger systems, which offset fixed soft costs faster, leading to quicker payback.

the studio homepage was built using a step-by-step grill-me interview to lock positioning, copy strategy, and design system before drafting assets. This upfront decision-making avoids rework and ensures alignment across teams.

## Why This Works

Top-performing ads often win because they're already proven. But their success is fragile. A structured creative process lets you dissect what works, then build better versions that go beyond imitation by layering in fresh proof and tighter execution.

This isn't about copying the best. It's about using a system to outperform them by being more disciplined, data-informed, and relentless in iteration.

## Creative Execution: What Makes It Stick

Creatives that win do so not just because of their message, but because of how they're built. The structure matters as much as the content.

- **Visual composition** must avoid three preventable issues: label readability (text on filled shapes fails), whitespace imbalance around headlines (even margins required), and blind overwrites of real photos (always check post intent first).
- **Profile pictures** render at ~40px next to ads. Two people in the image dissolve into illegibility and reduce trust. Use a single warm face. The original 1254x1254 frame failed the 40px test, which is the size the profile picture renders at next to every ad: his face came out about eight pixels tall and turned to mush.
- **Cover images** must be verified against a 2.628:1 desktop crop to ensure all key elements survive, and profile picture overlays should land on dark areas to avoid covering faces.
- **Ad code blocks** must not contain blank lines before closing lines. Meta inserts a "See More" cut at those points, visually detaching the CTA from the argument that earned it, which reduces conversion.
- **Primary text** must flow as one continuous thought. A blank line before a CTA gives Meta a place to insert the "See More" cut, visually detaching the ask from the argument that earned it.

The Storm Watch ad uses a plain, homemade-looking highlighter-yellow graphic with no design polish to signal authenticity and avoid appearing like a corporate sales pitch. It's deliberately imperfect.

## Narrative Strategy: How You Win the Mind

A winning narrative doesn't attack beliefs. It acknowledges them first.

The "village story" ad (Ad 3) uses a disclosed composite narrative that agrees with the market's outdated belief before showing why it no longer applies. It names the objection the whole market is holding, agrees with it, then shows why it stopped being true.

The myth-busting ad (Ad 4) reframes the outdated belief "solar doesn't pay" not as a falsehood but as a correct answer to a question that no longer applies. Three things moved: panels and batteries got dramatically cheaper, villa bills got bigger, and the goal shifted from selling power back to stopping the need to buy it.

A "three quotes relief" ad creative process works because it shows a homeowner comparing solar proposals with visible relief. It uses only generic documents and no branding to avoid bias, focusing purely on the decision-making experience.

## Authenticity & Visual Strategy

Hand-painted amateur photos of real objects (like bedsheet fences or cardboard in windows) stop scroll more effectively than designed graphics and avoid fatigue from repetition of yellow-square creatives. These are not polished. They're authentic. The Hurricane Tax ad round tests a new format: homemade signs on real objects in amateur photos, deliberately nothing like the yellow squares. The client Weaver's mechanic applies here: the message lives on a real hand-made object in a real photograph.

Raw and unpolished vertical walk-and-talk footage from live job sites consistently outperforms produced content for the solar installer audience. Documentary-style photography with authentic imperfections like phone-camera texture, natural skin, household clutter, and real lighting builds credibility and emotional relatability.

Villa photos with direct profit messaging outperform abstract financial graphics in ad creatives. The South target state solar image set uses cinematic realism with amplified physical details like magnified contract text or stacked bills to create scroll-stopping, photorealistic homeowner moments without branding or ad fluff.

The news-card creative is styled like a print newspaper with a masthead "SOUTHWEST the target state ENERGY REPORT" and a red bar chart showing the local utility's rate increase from 7.6¢ to 12.8¢ per kWh, reinforcing the credibility of the claim.

## Production & Technical Execution

The process for creating the solar installer Facebook group posts involves generating code-rendered hand-drawn graphics using HTML/SVG and rough.js, which are then rendered to PNG by headless Chrome for authenticity and cost efficiency. These nail the whiteboard/Excalidraw aesthetic that out-performs slick AI images on Facebook.

Dynamic Creative must be enabled at ad set level before any ad is created. Meta disables the toggle after an ad exists and forces a rebuild. This is a critical setup step.

Exact, legible numeric values such as $795.00, $130, $400, $357, $700/mo, 2.5% annual increase, and $108,000 must be displayed only as physical text in the scene. These numbers are not just data points; they're proof anchors.

A single hour of filming with a phone, tripod, and clip-on mic can produce one video per week for four to five weeks, with variations created in post-production using b-roll from an existing library. Every idea below is modeled on a video that is already working for a solar installer selling to consumers in another market: UK installer reels and Meta ads that have run profitably.

## Strategic Leverage & Decision-Making

the US solar company TPO lease video script was built by reverse-engineering a winning the target region solar ad, fact-checking claims against the local utility rate data and homeowner Reddit corpus, and confirming details with a client contact. This ensures the creative is grounded in real market behavior, not assumptions.

The new US solar company "Hurricane Tax" ad round used a five-question qualifying funnel model from the client Weaver's call as inspiration for the form structure. This aligns lead-gen flow with proven behavioral patterns.

The roofing campaign's hook was rebuilt using a fresh 90-day social pull that revealed homeowner anger over how adjusters count hail damage, leading to the adoption of the test square as a more credible and attention-grabbing element.

The decision to place capture-first CTAs on the green villa guide ensures lead attribution is secured, as untracked leads have zero comp value. Gated PDFs for form conversions are used to enforce this.

The operator killed seven of twelve v1 ad creatives because the constraint of "no faces, no legible text, no numbers" removed the only elements that stop a thumb scroll. the US solar company ads that worked do the exact opposite: `01-kitchen-shock` is a face, close, mid-reaction, holding a bill that reads TOTAL DUE $795.00 in type you can read at thumbnail size. `01-kitchen-projection` is a legal pad with $180/mo to $357/mo clearly visible.

The handwritten arithmetic on the legal pad creative uses correct math (8,000 W × $3.00 = $24,000 and 8,000 W × $5.00 = $40,000) to demonstrate real homeowner calculations.

At $270/month ad spend, infrastructure complexity for per-ad attribution exceeds ROI; phone-based matching provides 80% of value for 10% of work.
