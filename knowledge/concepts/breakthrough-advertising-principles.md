# Breakthrough Advertising: Schwartz's Core Principles

Advertising isn't about selling products. It's about channeling the massive, existing force of human desire and directing it toward a specific solution.

## The Core Insight: Market-Making, Not Selling

You don't create desire. You find it, focus it, and point it at your product. The economy runs on desire, not invention. Your job is to become its architect, not its originator.

"Advertising is the literature of desire."  
"The force that creates sales, that powers our present economy, is desire."

Copy cannot create desire for a product. It can only take the hopes, dreams, fears and desires that already exist in the hearts of millions of people, and focus those already-existing desires onto a particular product.

The copywriter does not create the desire of millions of women all over America to lose weight; but he can channel that desire onto a particular product, and make its owner a millionaire.

This is why advertising differs from education. Education must build desire from nothing. Advertising works with what's already there, which is why it scales so much faster and more powerfully.

## The Three Great Forces: Desire, Identification, Belief

Effective copy activates three interconnected psychological forces. All three must be present for a prospect to act.

### 1. Desire (Primary Force)

The raw hunger for change, relief, or transformation. It's not about the product itself, but what it promises.

- **Urgency:** How immediate is the need?
- **Staying power:** Is this desire fleeting or enduring?
- **Scope:** How widespread is it across your audience?

Your choice of which desire to exploit is the single most important decision in copywriting. Pick one that's already burning in people's minds.

### 2. Identification (Secondary Force)

People don't just buy products. They buy roles: who they want to be, how they want to be seen.

- **Character Roles:** Virile, modern, successful, adventurous.
- **Achievement Roles:** Status, class, recognition through possessions.

Build prestige by weaving favorable images into your product's identity. Not by claiming it outright, but by showing it through association and context.

"At least half of all purchases made today cannot be understood in terms of function alone."

### 3. Belief (Tertiary Force)

Your prospect lives by a set of unshakable beliefs about the world. These are not negotiable.

"If you violate your prospect's established beliefs in the slightest degree... then nothing you promise him, no matter how appealing, can save your ad."

Your job isn't to change belief. It's to align with it. Build a bridge from what they already believe to what they need to believe to act.

## The Six Mechanisms of Persuasion

### 1. Intensification

Show new, vivid images of satisfaction. Don't repeat the same scene. Vary the angle, context, and perspective. Reinforce desire through diversity, not repetition.

### 2. Identification (Building Prestige)

Weave social and character images into your product's story. Use visual cues more than words. Let the reader see themselves in the role your product enables.

### 3. Gradualization (Building Belief Structure)

Structure your copy so each claim leads naturally to the next. Start with something instantly believable, then build through a chain of acceptances.

Use inclusion questions, detailed identification, contradiction of false beliefs, and language of logic to make your argument feel inevitable.

### 4. Redefinition (Removing Preconceived Objections)

Don't argue with objections. Expand the frame. Show that the objection is part of a larger, more important issue.

- Simplify: Make complexity seem easy.
- Escalate: Show what's at stake beyond price or function.
- Reduce price: Compare to something far more expensive, not competitors.

### 5. Mechanization (Showing How It Works)

Explain the mechanism, but don't write a textbook. Load it with promise and emotion.

1. Name the mechanism  
2. Describe how it works  
3. Feature it as the reason to choose your product  

Every word must sell.

### 6. Concentration (Competitive Comparison)

Highlight weaknesses in alternatives only when you show how your product solves them. Frame it as a benefit to the prospect, not a jab at a rival.

## The Five Stages of Awareness

Your headline and approach change depending on where your audience is in their journey.

| Awareness Level | Lead Type | Headline Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Most aware (knows product, wants it) | Offer | Reinforce desire, show urgency | "50% off this weekend only" |
| Product known, skeptical | Promise | Name + superiority claim | "Get salon-quality blowouts in 1 hour" |
| Desire known, no product yet | Problem-Solution | Name problem, then deliver solution | "Remove dead skin that won't go away" |
| Problem known, solution vague | Problem-Agitate | Crystallize the pain | "Shrinks hemorrhoids without surgery" |
| Completely unaware | Proclamation | Call the market together | "For anyone tired of shaving" |

## The Juxtaposition Principle

One plus one can equal ten. When two powerful emotional images are combined at the right moment, their impact multiplies.

This is the highest form of creative copywriting: finding the right pairing that creates a new identity far more compelling than either image alone.

## The Headline's Role in Gradualization

Your headline must do two things:
1. Spark interest and desire
2. Seem believable at first glance

The strongest promise you can make may not be the one your prospect is ready to believe. Your headline's only job: get them to read the next sentence.

## Building the Architecture: Copy Flow

Anticipate what your reader will demand as they move through your copy:

1. **"Tell me more."** Feed their curiosity with new images.
2. **"Oh yeah? Who says so?"** Provide proof.
3. **"How does it work?"** Explain the mechanism.

Structure your copy to meet these demands at the moment they arise.

### The Complete Copy Architecture

1. Headline: Grab attention  
2. Opening copy: Reinforce desire and identity  
3. Product demonstration: Show it in action  
4. Multiple angles: Celebrity, expert, ordinary person  
5. Competitive contrast: Show your edge  
6. Heaven or Hell: Escape the pain, reach the reward  
7. Proof/Documentation: Testimonials, stats  
8. Mechanism: How it works  
9. Guarantee: Your confidence  
10. Close: Clear call to action

## Verification: Making Documentation Matter

Facts and stats don't sell on their own. You must build a drama around them.

Make documentation the hero that solves the reader's problem. Arrange it so it carries emotion, not just data.

"You have to develop a drama, in your reader's mind, into which your documentation enters as the hero."

## Three Fundamental Rules

### 1. Every Promise Needs Preparation

The believability of your claim depends on how well you've prepared your reader. The more preparation, the more acceptance.

### 2. Never Repeat, Always Reinforce

Repetition kills interest. Variation builds belief. Show the same core message through different images and angles.

### 3. Accept Reality, Don't Fight It

Advertising, like science, must accept reality as it is. Only then can you exploit its tendencies and give direction to its energy.

## The Danger: Believing Your Own Formula

"No formula works twice." Each formula is a solution to a past problem. Markets change. Problems evolve.

What will work? Innovation. Continuous, repeated innovation. A steady stream of fresh ideas, built not from memory but from analysis.

"The true copy writer must argue with success. He must push on past it every time he faces a new product."

## The Copy Writer's Primary Job

"The copy writer's primary job is to know his market."

This isn't about gut feeling. It's about engagement: listening, testing, analyzing, and learning from feedback.

You don't get a headline by inspiration. You build it, unfold it, work it out through research, product analysis, and hard thinking.

"You don't get an idea or a headline-you either build it, or you unfold it, petal by petal. You dig it out of the market research... you wring it out of the product... you work-hard."

This is the real discipline behind [[direct-response-advertising-methodology]] and [[ad-copy-testing-and-iteration]]. The best copy comes not from genius, but from deep understanding.
