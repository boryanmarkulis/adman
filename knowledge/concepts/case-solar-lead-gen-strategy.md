# Solar Lead Gen Strategy

A solar lead generation strategy that blends proven ad performance with experimental creative testing, automated follow-up systems, and a scalable partner network to drive consistent, high-quality leads.

## Ad Spend Allocation and Creative Testing

We split ad spend 70/30: 70% goes to creatives that are already proven to perform, while 30% is allocated to experimental or viral-style content. This allows us to maintain a steady flow of qualified leads while testing new formats and messaging without risking the core campaign performance. The key metric we track is click-through rate, which helps us quickly identify what's resonating and where to shift budget.

This approach isn't about chasing virality for its own sake. It's about using controlled experimentation to uncover new high-performing angles that can eventually be scaled into the main funnel.

## Automated Lead Follow-Up

Every lead gets an automated message within five minutes of submission. The first message confirms their address and triggers a simple yes/no response, which then kicks off a sequence of follow-up messages. This system reduces manual work, ensures no leads slip through, and maintains momentum in the early stages of the sales funnel.

The automation isn't just about speed. It's about consistency. Every lead gets the same clear, professional touchpoint, which builds trust and sets expectations early.

## Partner Network Model

We scale by bringing in partners who either front ad spend or agree to split commissions. Once a partner demonstrates they can convert leads into appointments, we launch a dedicated campaign for them. This model reduces our upfront risk while expanding reach through trusted teams on the ground.

It also creates accountability. Partners are incentivized to perform because their success directly impacts their earnings.

## Hybrid Sales Model

We use both in-house and virtual sales reps. In-home sales reps convert at around 45%, while virtual teams average 20% to 25%. Though conversion rates are lower, the virtual model is still profitable due to lower overhead and faster scaling.

The hybrid approach lets us maintain high close rates for complex deals while using remote reps to handle volume and initial qualification.

## Relationship-First Outreach

Building relationships with solar pros is foundational. It took over a year to learn where they hang out online (Facebook groups) and how to connect without pitching immediately. Trust comes before transaction.

This network access isn't just for leads. It's for insights, referrals, and long-term collaboration that fuel the entire lead generation engine.

## Market Context and Offer Nuance

Homeowners who finance solar installations through loans may face challenges when selling their homes, as new buyers must qualify for the same loan and lenders often resist solar-related debt. This is a real barrier that impacts buyer perception and transaction velocity.

On the other hand, community solar programs in the target state offer a low-barrier alternative: residents can buy into a shared solar farm without rooftop panels, long-term contracts, or equipment. Renters can participate too. This model expands access beyond traditional homeownership and opens new customer segments for outreach.

## TPO / PPO Education Frame

In the target state, a high-converting ad angle reframes solar leases as a "Third Party Ownership program" (also called PPO locally) run in response to the state's energy crisis. The hook: the government enables programs where homeowners don't own the equipment, just get a lower electric bill. The advertiser is not selling solar -- they are educating people on a program they may already qualify for.

This frame shifts the emotional register from "sales pitch" to "public service," reduces objection before the first conversation, and maps cleanly to door-knocking scripts where reps present as educators, not closers. Field-tested with the client's the US solar company team in the target metro (2026-08-20).

## Data Source Strategy

Using free county tax roll data instead of scraping Zillow or buying third-party leads provides complete, legal, and higher-quality data without blocking risks or monthly costs. This approach avoids compliance issues and ensures we're working with accurate, up-to-date information.

Free public county data and open city APIs beat commercial scrapers in completeness, legal safety, and data quality. The target metro's ArcGIS permit API only covers installations from late 2024 onward, so older solar homes remain on the knock list and are filtered in-person by knockers.

## Strategic Insights from Real-World Feedback

Solar companies that target vulnerable populations with complex PPAs may face reputational and legal risks, especially when systems are installed on roofs with insufficient remaining lifespan. Salespeople who push these deals on elderly or financially strained customers are widely criticized as unethical.

Aggressive lead generation tactics, including door-to-door outreach in high-need areas like food pantries, have damaged industry trust. Misleading promises about savings and long wait times for panel installation are common complaints.

The failure of large-scale providers like Tesla to deliver on promised installations has created a market opportunity for local installers who offer reliable service, transparent pricing, and responsive post-installation support.

## Financial and Behavioral Drivers

The decision to go solar should be based on long-term financial and energy independence rather than short-term price fluctuations. Waiting for lower prices may result in missing out on significant tax credits and utility rate increases.

Solar system payback periods can be shortened by using excess production to charge an electric vehicle. One homeowner noted that adding an EV would put their surplus solar generation to good use and increase returns.

The financial return on a solar investment can also be enhanced by selling excess electricity at a premium during peak demand periods. In some areas, utilities charge up to 60 cents per kWh during cloudy or high-use days.

In the second state, installing a solar system with a 4% Mass Solar Loan results in a monthly payment of $250, which is only slightly higher than the previous electric bill, making the financial transition manageable and a net gain over time.
