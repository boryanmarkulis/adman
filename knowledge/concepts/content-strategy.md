# Content Strategy

A content strategy is about creating material that speaks directly to your super consumer, using tools and insights to stay relevant and effective.

## Speak to Your Super Consumer

Your content should be laser-focused on your super consumer. This isn't just a demographic; it's the person who engages deeply, buys consistently, and believes in your mission. When you know them inside out, every piece of content must either resonate with them or get scrapped. Anything that doesn't serve this core audience isn't worth your time.

## Use AI to Source and Shape Content

Leverage AI tools like GPT to quickly find relevant information, especially around high-intent topics like instant hair loss solutions or hair extensions. These aren't just research aids; they're part of your content pipeline, helping you stay ahead on trending topics and emerging needs in your niche. You can prompt AI to source articles directly, such as asking for content on "instant hair loss solutions," and use the results to inform your messaging and structure.

## Platforms Are Built for Precision

Social platforms don't push content randomly. They serve it based on user interests and behaviors. Your strategy must align with this reality. The more precisely your content matches what your super consumer is already searching for or engaging with, the more naturally it will spread through organic reach.

## Stay Focused, Not Busy

Don't create content just because you can. Every post, video, or article should pass the test: does this speak to my super consumer? If not, it doesn't belong in your strategy. This focus is what turns noise into signal.

## Build Around a Core Framework

A strong content strategy often starts with a shared core document that can be forked into multiple outputs. For example, the a reference project case study was built around one master outline covering key systems like energy (AC, insulation, orientation), water and waste, PV sizing, and material choices. From this single foundation, three distinct assets were created: the case study, an off-grid guide, and a green building guide.

## Prioritize Speed to Market

Not all content pieces have the same launch timeline. The off-grid guide was prioritized for fast publication because it had no external dependencies, unlike the co-branded case study which required coordination with a partner studio. When speed matters, identify and publish the asset that can be delivered first without waiting on others.

## Anchor on a Powerful, Verified Claim

The most compelling marketing claim should be backed by clear methodology. In the a reference project case, the figure of 1,922 t CO2eq avoided across 176 projects became the centerpiece of outreach. It was validated using accurate grid emission factors (0.625 kg CO2eq/kWh for the local market, 1.13 for the local market), making it credible and trustworthy for public use.
