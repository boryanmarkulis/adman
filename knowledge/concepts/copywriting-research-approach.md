# Copywriting Research Approach

Effective copy starts not with a blank page, but with deep, structured research into real customer voices and behaviors.

## Gather Raw Customer Feedback

The foundation of powerful copy is direct input from customers. This means collecting every available piece of feedback: product reviews, ad comments, email responses, social media mentions, and survey results. These aren't just data points; they're the raw language people use when describing their problems, desires, and frustrations.

A strong research practice involves building a central reference document, often a Google Doc that can grow to 40 to 100 pages. This isn't about summarizing or filtering early on. It's about capturing everything verbatim so you don't lose the texture of real human expression.

## Organize for Insight, Not Just Volume

Having mountains of feedback is useless unless you can turn it into clarity. The goal isn't to polish or rewrite the customer voice, but to *listen deeply* and reassemble their words into a message that feels authentic and understood. You're not speaking *for* them; you're speaking *with* them.

This means reading through hundreds of reviews not to find patterns, but to feel the rhythm of their pain and desire. The most compelling copy emerges when you reflect back what people are already saying, just in a clearer, more urgent form.

## Prioritize Real-Time Data Over Assumptions

While forums and online discussions can help with early-stage ideation, they often reveal opinions shaped by theory or secondhand experience rather than actual behavior. The real gold lies in data that comes from people who are actively engaging with your brand: those who've clicked, commented, bought, or left a review.

These interactions are signals of attention and intent. They show what people care about *right now*, not what they think they should care about. That's why direct customer feedback is more valuable than any hypothetical market research.

## Focus on Pain Points and Language That Resonates

The most effective research isn't broad; it's surgical. Look for recurring themes in reviews and ad comments: the same phrases people use to describe their frustration, the exact words they use when they finally feel relief. These are your copy goldmines.

When you find a phrase that appears again and again in customer feedback, don't just note it. Use it. Let it guide your headlines, your offer structure, your emotional hooks. The language that resonates isn't invented; it's already out there, waiting to be discovered.

## Balance Research with Writing

The best results come from a disciplined rhythm: spend 80% of your time researching and refining insights, 20% writing and testing. This isn't about perfection before creation. It's about grounding every word in real data so that your first draft is already closer to the truth.

This 80-20 balance prevents guesswork and ensures that every line of copy is rooted in what actual customers are saying and feeling.

## The Compounding Power of Deep Research

When you consistently gather, organize, and act on customer insights, you unlock compounding growth. You stop targeting "everyone" and start serving one specific person with precision. This clarity leads to higher conversion rates, better ad performance, and stronger brand loyalty.

This is where businesses move from seven to eight or nine figures: not through more ads, but through deeper understanding. The more you listen, the less you need to explain. The message becomes inevitable.
