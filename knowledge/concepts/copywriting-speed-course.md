# Copywriting Speed Course

Great sales copy doesn't just inform. It makes people *feel* the benefit and believe it's real, fast.

## Specificity Builds Trust

Generic claims fade into the background. When you say "this product works," no one remembers. But when you say "it's so effective, you don't even have to come close to hitting your 'target' area... You can literally see that 160lb bar pounding right into a bad guy's belly," people *see* it. Specificity isn't just detail for detail's sake. It signals honesty and precision. It tells the reader, "I know what I'm talking about." Platitudes wash away like water off a duck. Specifics stick.

## Verbs Are Your Show-Off Words

Short, sharp verbs do the heavy lifting. They create motion, tension, and impact. "Drop opponents instantly" isn't just a phrase. It's a physical sensation. It makes you *feel* the force. Verbs are where the energy lives. Use them like punches. Not every word needs to be fancy. The right one at the right time cuts deeper than a whole paragraph of fluff.

## Speak to Both Heart and Mind

People buy with emotion, but they need logic to justify the purchase. Your copy can't just tug at heartstrings. It also has to give them a reason to pull the trigger. That means balancing emotional triggers like fear, desire, or relief with clear, rational support. The best copy makes you *want* it and then says, "And yes, this is actually how it works."

## Use the Language of Credibility

Credibility isn't built on hype. It's built on tone. Using words that sound scientific, precise, or grounded in logic helps the reader trust the claim. Phrases like "the 'language of logic'" aren't about being cold. They're about sounding like someone who knows their stuff. That subtle shift makes all the difference between a sales pitch and a trusted recommendation.

## Mental Images Are Your Weapon

The goal isn't to describe a product. It's to make the reader *experience* it in their mind. Vivid, sensory language turns abstract benefits into something real. When you can picture the 160lb bar hitting its mark, you're not just reading copy. You're already imagining yourself using the product. That mental image is what drives action.
