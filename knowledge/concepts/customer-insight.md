# Customer insight

Customer insights are the raw material of real-world messaging and product refinement, turning genuine user voices into strategic direction.

## Real voices drive better copy

The best ad copy doesn't come from internal brainstorming or assumptions. It comes from listening to what people actually say. When users on TikTok described a product's packaging as "cute," that exact phrase became the foundation for headlines and messaging. Instead of inventing tone, teams pull real language from customer comments to build authenticity and resonance. This isn't just about wording; it's about capturing the actual rhythm and emotion people use when they talk about something they like.

## Feedback shapes both product and message

Insights aren't just about copy. They influence how a product is positioned, what value it highlights, and even how it's designed. The feedback on packaging cuteness didn't stop at ad headlines. It helped define the brand's identity around charm and relatability, shaping everything from visuals to tone of voice. When customers react to something as simple as packaging, it reveals deeper emotional connections that can guide broader product decisions.

## Listening is strategic

It's not enough to hear customers. You have to go out and listen in real contexts. Walking through neighborhoods and talking to people face-to-face reveals a spectrum of perspectives on solar energy, from deep skepticism to active adoption. These unfiltered conversations expose emotional triggers, hidden objections, and natural language that no survey or focus group can replicate.

## Direct outreach uncovers diversity of thought

One-on-one conversations reveal more than data points. They show how personal values, life stages, and local context shape attitudes toward energy choices. A few hours of walking and talking with 10-15 people uncovered entirely different worldviews, proving that customer insight isn't a single story but a mosaic of lived experience.

## Defense is often about history, not the message

People who react defensively to outreach are often responding to past negative experiences with similar services, not the current message itself. Many have been burned by previous solar programs, and some even view the entire category with suspicion. Recognizing this helps shift the focus from persuasion to empathy. The goal isn't to win an argument but to understand where someone is coming from before offering a solution.
