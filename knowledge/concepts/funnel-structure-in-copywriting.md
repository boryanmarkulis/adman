# Funnel structure in copywriting

Copy should guide readers through a clear, narrowing path from awareness to action, like a funnel that starts wide and tightens toward the goal.

## The funnel as a mental model

Think of your copy like an upside-down triangle. It begins broad, capturing attention with a wide-open idea or problem. As the reader moves down, each section narrows focus, eliminating distractions and guiding them toward a single, clear next step. This isn't just about layout; it's about logic. Every line should pull the reader forward, reducing ambiguity and building momentum.

## How it works in practice

Start with a hook that speaks to a universal pain or desire. Then layer in context, proof, and urgency. Each stage removes friction: you explain the problem, show why it matters, prove your solution works, and finally make the ask. The structure isn't rigid, but it must feel inevitable. By the time they reach the call to action, the reader has already decided to act.

## Why it beats random rambling

Without a funnel, copy feels scattered. Readers get lost or disengage. A funnel forces clarity. It ensures every sentence earns its place by advancing the journey. This isn't just about persuasion; it's about respect for the reader's time and attention.
