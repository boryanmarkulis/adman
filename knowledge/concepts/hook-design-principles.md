# Hook Design Principles

Great hooks grab attention by disrupting expectations, repeating key lines for memorability, and pairing words with strong visuals or audio.

## Jarring or Shocking Element

A hook should surprise the viewer. It doesn't have to be extreme, but it needs to break the pattern of what's expected. This could be a bold statement, an unexpected image, or a sudden shift in tone. The goal is to make the brain stop scrolling and say, "Wait, what?"

## Repetition of the First Sentence

Repeat the opening line within the first few seconds. This isn't just for emphasis; it's about anchoring the message in memory. When viewers hear the same idea again shortly after, it sticks better. It also gives the hook rhythm and weight.

## Visual or Audio Component

Words alone aren't enough. The hook must have a matching visual or audio cue that reinforces the message. This could be b-roll footage, text animation, sound effects, or music. The screen should *show* what the words say, creating a unified sensory punch.

These three elements together create a hook that cuts through noise and demands attention. Use them intentionally, not just for effect.
