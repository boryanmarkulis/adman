# Hook Prompt Categories

A taxonomy of 10 proven hook types for short-form content, designed to break creative blocks and ensure you're always pulling the right psychological lever.

## Why This Exists

Most creators fall into a rut, relying on just one or two hook styles. This framework forces variety by assigning each piece of content to a specific category. It's not about being clever; it's about being strategic. Each category targets a different mental trigger: curiosity, social proof, efficiency, pattern recognition, or controversy. Knowing which lever you're pulling makes writing faster and testing more precise.

## The 10 Categories

**1. Big Premise.** Bold, controversial claims that challenge what your audience already believes. Think "This one habit ruined my life" or "The solar industry is lying to you." These hooks work because they create instant tension: *Is this true? I need to find out.*

**2. Social Collaboration.** Unscripted interactions with others: challenges, public reactions, co-creation. The hook is the invitation itself. People tune in not for perfection, but for real-time dynamics and surprise.

**3. Challenge / Storytelling.** Personal experiments, family projects, DIYs, or failure stories. The creator is the subject, and the outcome matters. Real stakes = real engagement. Failure lessons are especially powerful because they deliver value through vulnerability.

**4. Big Benefit.** "Big result, little effort." Hooks that highlight automation, meal prep hacks, or tools that save hours. Taps into the universal desire to do more without burning out.

**5. Puzzles.** Questions, quizzes, paradoxes, WMTD (What Made Them Different) scenarios. These force the brain to pause and engage. The payoff? Solving it before the video ends.

**6. Patterns.** Odd connections, metaphor bridges, surprising origins. "What do solar panels and spaghetti have in common?" The unexpected link is the hook. It plays on curiosity and cognitive surprise.

**7. New Information.** Tips, how-tos, common mistakes, uncommon solutions, efficiency hacks. Straight-up useful knowledge. Works best when it feels exclusive or counterintuitive. Shareability spikes here.

**8. Insight.** The "why" behind the "what." Science, myth-busting, expert perspectives, hidden influences, secret history, deceptive practices. This builds authority and trust. Q&A formats fit perfectly here.

**9. Comparison.** Head-to-head product tests, alternative options, unexpected winners. Clear stakes from the first frame. Great for e-commerce, decision fatigue, or side-hustle validation.

**10. Odd / Entertaining / Bizarre.** Weird products, absurd premises, kitchen experiments. No deeper purpose than novelty and fun. Low effort to create, but when it lands, it can go viral.

## How to Use the Framework

Pick a category before you write. Write three hooks from different categories for the same piece of content. Then choose the one that matches your audience's current awareness level. New audiences need Big Premise or Odd to break through. Warmer audiences respond better to Insight or Comparison.
