# Landing Page Edits

Small, intentional changes to the landing page can dramatically improve clarity, trust, and conversion.

## Visual Clarity and Spacing

Spacing isn't just empty room. It's breathing space for the user. Tight layouts overwhelm. Clean spacing guides the eye and makes content feel intentional. Mobile views especially need generous padding and clear visual separation between sections. Removing unnecessary links or elements helps reduce noise and keeps focus on what matters.

## Headlines and Subheadings

Headlines should deliver the core promise in one breath. They aren't summaries or filler. The best ones cut straight to the point, explaining the most important thing a visitor needs to know. Subheadings then support that idea with just enough context to build curiosity without confusion.

## Body Copy Focus

Less is more. Over-explaining kills momentum. The body copy should be razor-sharp, highlighting only the key benefits and differentiators. Every sentence must earn its place. If it doesn't serve the core message or remove a barrier, it belongs in a footnote or a follow-up page.

## Layout and Flow

The layout should guide the user naturally from headline to offer to action. This means aligning visual elements with intent: buttons where they're expected, images where they support the story. Adjustments like centering, resizing, or repositioning can fix awkward flows and make the page feel more polished and intentional.

## Real Customer Proof

Authenticity beats polish. Using real customer reviews and photos builds trust faster than any staged testimonial. Fake-looking quotes feel like marketing noise. Real ones feel like proof. Make sure they're visible, readable, and clickable on both desktop and mobile.

## Button and Image Visibility

Buttons are the call to action. If they're small, poorly placed, or hard to tap, users won't engage. Make them big enough to tap easily, especially on mobile. Images should be clear, relevant, and positioned to support the message, not distract from it. Their placement should reinforce the flow of the page.

## Mobile-First Testing

A landing page isn't complete until it works well on a phone. What looks great on desktop can feel cramped or broken on mobile. Test every element: clickable areas, font size, image scaling, and button visibility. If something isn't clickable on mobile, fix it. The user experience starts where the screen ends.
