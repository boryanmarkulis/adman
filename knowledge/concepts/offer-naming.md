# Offer Naming

Naming your offer right turns it from a vague idea into something people can't ignore.

## Core vs. Marketing Names

Your core offer name should stay consistent over time. It's the brand anchor, the thing worth trademarking. Think iPhone, MacBook, Model 3, or Media Scaling System. This name builds long-term recognition and trust. Don't change it just to test a new angle.

Marketing offers, on the other hand, are temporary campaigns. They use catchy names to attract attention and drive action. These can vary widely and don't need to match your core brand name exactly.

## The MAGIC Formula

Use this proven structure to craft irresistible offer names:

- **M** - Make a magnetic reason why: Start with a powerful benefit that grabs attention.
- **A** - Announce your avatar: Clearly state who the offer is for.
- **G** - Give them a goal: Name a specific, tangible outcome they want.
- **I** - Indicate a time interval: Set a clear timeframe to expect results (shorter is better).
- **C** - Complete with a container word: End with a category or format like "system," "program," or "challenge."

Example: *Double Your Lead Flow in 30 Days: The 2026 Lead Surge System for Solopreneurs*

## Specificity Wins

The goal in your offer name must be concrete. Vague promises like "grow faster" don't land. Instead, use outcomes your ideal customer dreams about: lose 10 pounds, save 10 hours a week, get a 20% raise, triple your profit.

This specificity builds credibility and makes the promise feel real.

## Time Intervals Create Urgency

Always include a time frame. It gives clarity and pressure. The shorter the better. Use seconds, minutes, days, weeks, or months. This helps people visualize success and act fast.
