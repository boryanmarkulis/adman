# Product-Market Fit Testing

Testing for product-market fit isn't just about validating demand; it's about proving that your offering delivers real value in a way people are willing to pay for.

## Value Perception Is the Core Filter

Even if a product is technically solid, it can fail in testing if users don't perceive enough value relative to its price. The gap between what you think the product is worth and what the market believes it's worth is where most early-stage failures happen. A niche audience might be interested, but if they don't feel the payoff justifies the cost, engagement stalls.

## Market-Specific Testing Yields Real Signals

The operator tested around ten different solar markets before finding a segment that responded with high intent. He achieved a 50% engagement rate and secured site visits from the first 30 messages sent to one specific group. This shows that fit isn't about broad appeal but about precision: identifying the right audience, messaging, and price point combination through direct testing.

## Testing Isn't About Scale, It's About Signal

You don't need thousands of users to know if you're on track. A small, focused test with clear outcomes can reveal whether your product resonates. The goal isn't volume; it's validation. When engagement spikes and real actions follow (like site visits), that's the signal that fit is possible.
