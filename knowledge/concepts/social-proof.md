# Social Proof

Social proof builds trust by showing that others have already tried and validated a product or idea.

## How It Works in Practice

People are more likely to act when they see others doing the same. In advertising, this means using real customer testimonials, visible usage patterns, or social activity to signal legitimacy. For example, when someone shares that they bought a product because it was trending on their for you page, that moment of decision becomes proof that the product is worth trying.

## Real-World Application

A strong social proof strategy doesn't rely on fake engagement. Instead, it highlights authentic moments like real-time purchases, visible customer activity, or unfiltered reviews. These signals carry more weight than polished ads because they reflect actual behavior in real communities.

## Real-World Application

A strong social proof strategy doesn't rely on fake engagement. Instead, it highlights authentic moments like real-time purchases, visible customer activity, or unfiltered reviews. These signals carry more weight than polished ads because they reflect actual behavior in real communities.

Posts that create organic conversation about electricity issues like blackouts or bill shocks generate more engagement than direct sales pitches, even when posted from the brand page. This shows that social proof isn't just about testimonials or promotions. It's also about sparking real conversations around shared pain points. The act of asking questions about energy bills or power outages can trigger peer validation and collective problem-solving, which in turn builds trust in the brand as a helpful, relatable voice.
