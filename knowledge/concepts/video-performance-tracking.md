# Video Performance Tracking

Tracking video performance isn't optional. It's how you learn what works and where to improve.

## Why Track Video Performance?

You can't optimize what you don't measure. Every video post is a data point in your content strategy. Without consistent tracking, you're guessing instead of learning. The goal isn't just to count views, but to understand which videos drive engagement, retention, and conversions so you can double down on what works.

## Tools That Help

Platforms like Metricool make it easy to monitor performance across social channels. They let you see view counts, completion rates, shares, and comments in one place. This gives you a clear picture of which content resonates and which falls flat. Use these tools not just for reporting, but as part of your weekly review ritual.

## How to Track Consistently

Set a recurring task to review video performance every week. Go through each post from the past seven days. Note which videos got the most views, which had high retention, and which underperformed. Use this data to refine your hooks, pacing, and CTAs. Over time, patterns emerge that reveal what your audience truly responds to.

## Linking to Other Workflows

This practice ties directly into [[short-form-video-retention]], [[ad-campaign-optimization]], and content creator performance tracking. When you track video performance, you're feeding real-time data into broader content and ad strategies. It also supports [[video-editing-workflow]] by showing what edits actually move the needle.

## Don't Just Track, Act

Tracking is only valuable if you act on it. If a video with a certain hook performs well, reuse that structure. If a format consistently underperforms, test a new approach. The cycle of tracking → analyzing → iterating is how content evolves from good to great.
