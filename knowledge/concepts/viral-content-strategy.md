# Viral Content Strategy

Find the formats that work for your brand, then repeat them until they stop working.

## The Core Loop

Top brands in the business category don't constantly reinvent. They find two or three video formats that convert, and they run them on rotation. The whole game is identifying those formats faster than you would by guessing. The method: test multiple formats each month, track what gets shares and saves, cut the losers, double the winners.

Instagram's own head (Adam Mosseri) confirmed the two metrics that matter most: views to shares and views to saves. Everything else is noise.

The same principle applies to sourcing: find what's already working in your niche and do it better. Don't start from scratch when someone else has already proven the concept.

## Finding Winning Formats

Use Instagram search on mobile, not desktop. Only the mobile app shows video view counts, which is what you need to spot outliers.

The 5x rule: if an account has 1M followers and a post got 5M views, that's worth studying even if the follower count is large. You're looking for posts that dramatically outperformed the account's baseline, not just raw view numbers.

The fastest way to find winning viral formats is to test multiple formats each month. This isn't about guessing or waiting for inspiration. It's a systematic process of trial and refinement.

## Format Patterns That Work

A numbered series builds compounding momentum. Post 135 of a series still gets views because the format trains the algorithm and the audience simultaneously. You can do this by episode number, "Day X of Y," or part numbers.

Unique hook, standard close. Write a fresh top one to three lines for every post, but use a templated call to action at the bottom. It saves time and trains your audience on what to expect.

Multi-part series and numbered sequences are also good anchor points for [[content-strategy]] across accounts.

## Production Details That Move Metrics

**Captions:** Show one to three words at a time. The 2-second rule keeps text popping constantly, which holds attention better than eight-word blocks sitting static on screen.

**Fonts:** Bolder and heavier reads cleaner. Thin fonts look weak. Avoid strong shadows or heavy borders around text.

**Authority banner:** Put your biggest credential on screen, whatever it is. "Award-winning author," "50MW installed," whatever is real and specific. Credibility cues reduce scroll-past.

**2x speed animation:** A small "2x" animation pop-up signals to viewers they can speed-watch, and that actually improves retention on mobile.

**Music:** Should always sit under the speaker, never over them. If you can't clearly hear every word, the music is too loud.

**Audio editing:** Cut filler words, dead air, repeated phrases. Training a consistent audio track helps with brand recognition but is secondary to the cuts.

**Final review:** Watch the whole video before it goes to the scheduler. Editors have a habit of queueing without watching. One full pass catches most errors.

## The Underlying Principle

Testing formats is not a one-time project. It's an ongoing loop that compounds. Brands that win long-term have systematized this: source formats, test them, identify outliers by shares and saves, retire what's flat, promote what's working. The goal is a repeatable machine, not a lucky post.

The operator uses a strategy of replicating viral content in his niche by finding and reusing successful scripts and video footage. The best way to go viral is like the best way to find a new store: by finding something that's working and just doing it but doing it better.
