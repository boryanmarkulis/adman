# Black Friday Scaling Rules

## The Setup
Black Friday is not a normal week. It is a leverage window.

The point is not to work harder in a vague way. The point is to lock the offer, build the list, and put more budget behind what already works.

For a seasonal brand, Q4 can carry the whole year.

## Finalize The Offer
Do not wait for Black Friday to invent the offer. Build it before the day arrives.

The offer should be tested against:
- Existing buyers
- Retargeting audiences
- Cold traffic at a low spend
- Email samples or list segments

The main move is to build a [[collection-offer-architecture]] that lifts AOV and gives people more ways to buy again. A single product is often too narrow. A collection can catch more demand.

## Scale Buyers First
Previous customers are the easiest money on Black Friday.

If you already have buyers, talk to them first.
If you already have trust, use it.
If you already know what they like, give them more of that.

The order of attack:
- Buyers list
- Retargeting
- Warm audiences
- Cold traffic

A lot of people forget that the list is the asset. Existing customers can carry the biggest days if the offer is real.

## Day-Of Rules
Black Friday itself is the big day. That is where the hardest push belongs.

Rules:
- Spend hardest on Friday
- Use the winning creative only
- Start with the strongest audiences first
- Pull back if the campaign overshoots
- Do not waste the whole weekend pretending every day is equal

Saturday and Sunday matter less.
Cyber Monday can catch some of the overflow.
Then you tighten back up again.

The mistake is scaling too cautiously on Friday and too aggressively after the day is already behind you, the same discipline [[green-means-scale-red-means-no]] is built on.

## The Core Principle

Black Friday is the peak. Scale hardest on that day, then pull back through the weekend. Don't treat the whole window as equal. The spike in CPMs and buyer intent happens only once. That's when you send it. The rest is cleanup and learning.

## Offer Strategy

Push average order value (AOV) like it's your job. CPMs explode during BFCM because everyone's spending. If your offer doesn't lift AOV, you get squeezed. Build a collection page with your new products and test combinations until one sticks. Use your buyer email list to test offers in batches of a thousand. When one converts, scale it hard into Black Friday.

## Media Buying Approach

Focus on what already works. The pre-BFCM weeks are for discovery, not execution. BFCM is for scaling proven angles. Start with higher-than-comfortable budgets because pulling back is easier than adding mid-campaign. Delayed attribution means you can't catch up if you under-spend early.

A big CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization) focused on retargeting existing buyers is worth testing. Converting past customers is cheaper and faster when CPMs are high. The goal isn't just acquisition, it's increasing lifetime value (LTV).

## Email as a Testing Ground

Your buyer list is your lab. Use it to test offer frames before spending on ads. Send samples to 1,000 buyers at a time. Watch which version converts. Once you find it, blast the full list and back it with paid media.

## Competitor Research

Keep your eyes open every day. You won't find opportunities by sitting still. The best offers, bundles, and collection structures come from watching what competitors are doing. I found one of my top-performing systems by reverse-engineering a competitor's setup through ad buying and market research.

## Mindset

This is the most compressed learning cycle of the year. You'll make mistakes. You'll overspend or leave money on the table. That's okay. The real win isn't this year's profit. It's next year's clarity. Treat it like a bossy day with no rules. Be bold. Be fast. Learn hard.
