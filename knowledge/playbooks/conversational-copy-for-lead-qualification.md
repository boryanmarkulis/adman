# Conversational Copy for Lead Qualification

WhatsApp, SMS, and chat-based lead qualification requires different copy rules than ads or landing pages. The conversation must feel natural while extracting the information your sales team needs.

## The Core Principle: Chat Feels Like Texting a Friend

People immediately sense corporate scripting on WhatsApp. They close the app. Your job is to qualify leads through natural conversation, not interrogation.

Aria (the solar installer's lead qualification bot) demonstrates this: short messages, warm tone, no filler, questions asked naturally one at a time.

## The Rules

### Message Length

Keep messages under 20 words when possible. Short bursts feel like real texting. Long blocks feel automated.

Weak: "Thank you for reaching out to the solar installer! We're excited to learn more about your solar needs. Could you please provide us with your name, location, and the type of property you own so we can better assist you?"

Strong: "Hey! Thanks for reaching out. What's your name?"

Then wait for their reply before asking the next thing.

### Tone: Warm, Not Corporate

Warm language:
- "Got it!"
- "Cool!"
- "Got everything I need!"
- "Sorry about that!" (instead of "I apologize")
- Use first names once you have them
- Use regional honorifics ("Kak" in Bahasa the local market)

Corporate language to avoid:
- "I appreciate your inquiry"
- "We appreciate the opportunity"
- "At your earliest convenience"
- "Please advise"
- "Filler apologies" ("I sincerely apologize for any inconvenience")

### Never Sound Robotic

Robotic triggers:
- Every message sounds the same
- Perfect grammar in every sentence
- Filler phrases ("As mentioned above," "Please note that")
- Formal sign-offs
- Emojis used systematically (every message gets one)

Natural signs:
- Occasional casual grammar ("Got it" vs "I understand")
- Variety in message structure
- Real closing signals ("Talk soon!" vs "Sincerely,")
- Selective emoji use (only when it serves the message, not as punctuation)

## The Qualification Flow

### Core Information (Required Before Handoff)

1. Name
2. What they need (solar install, buy panels, something else)
3. Location (city/region)
4. Property type (home, villa, hotel, restaurant, etc.)
5. How they heard about you

These five pieces let your sales team follow up effectively. Don't try to get more.

### Bonus Information (If Conversation Flows Naturally)

6. Electricity bill (monthly cost)
7. Email address
8. Installation timeline
9. Battery/off-grid preference

Key phrase: "only if the conversation flows naturally, never force these."

Forcing bonus questions kills the natural feel. You're qualifying, not interrogating.

## Handling Different Scenarios

### Product Questions Beyond Your Scope

If they ask technical questions about system design, pricing, or feasibility, don't answer. Hand off to the team.

**EN:** "That's a great question! Our team will go into all the details when they reach out."

**ID:** "Pertanyaan bagus! Tim kami akan jelaskan detailnya saat mereka menghubungi Kakak."

### Requests You Don't Handle

If they ask for something outside your scope (job applications, component sales, competitor promotions):

**Component sales:** "We're an installation company, so we don't sell individual panels. Check with local solar distributors in your area for that."

**Job applications:** "Thanks! Send your CV to [email] and the team will take it from there."

**Spam/promotions:** One-line polite decline or ignore. Don't engage further.

### Existing Customers

If they're an existing customer with after-sales needs:

**EN:** "Hey! Thanks for reaching out. Just to confirm - what's your name and where did we install your system?"

Then hand off to the team with the context.

## Hard Rules

These are absolute:

1. **Never quote prices.** "That depends on your system size. The team will give you an exact quote when they call."

2. **Never make technical claims.** Stick to facts. If you don't know, the team will answer.

3. **Never commit to a visit or installation date.** "The team will discuss timing when they reach out."

4. **Always set expectation of human follow-up.** End every conversation with a clear signal that a human will contact them soon.

5. **Hand off if a human joins the conversation.** If a team member starts messaging, stop. The human has taken over.

## Language Switching

If the customer switches languages mid-conversation, follow them.

If they start in Bahasa the local market, respond in Bahasa the local market. If they switch to English, switch to English.

This feels natural. Forcing them to your preferred language feels robotic.

## Why This Works

Leads qualified through natural conversation feel less sold. They're giving you information because the exchange felt human, not because they were interrogated.

When your sales team calls, they already have context. The conversation felt good. The lead is warmer.

Comparison:

**Robotic bot:** Customer fills out a form, feels impersonal, closes the app, stops responding.

**Conversational bot:** Customer texts naturally, feels like talking to a person, stays engaged, is ready for the sales call.

The difference compounds. Better qualification leads to better leads passed to sales, which leads to higher close rates, the same design behind the CRM solar whatsapp agent.

## Principles from Conversational Copy

These apply everywhere conversation happens (WhatsApp, SMS, email, support chat):

1. **Short messages beat long ones.** Attention on chat is fractured. Respect it with brevity.

2. **Questions asked naturally feel less like interrogation.** One question at a time. Wait for the answer. Then the next.

3. **Tone-matching builds trust.** If they're casual, be casual. If they're formal, adapt slightly.

4. **Warmth is your brand on chat,** the same authenticity rule from [[personality-in-copy]]. People remember how you made them feel, not what you said.

5. **Transparency removes friction.** "The team will call you tomorrow" beats vague follow-ups.
