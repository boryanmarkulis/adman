# Copywriting Mechanics

A practical playbook for the small moves that make copy feel believable, human, and persuasive.

## Lower Defenses
Copy works better when it feels like useful advice from a real person, the same voice principle [[claude-hopkins-principles]] argues for.
- Warm, casual tone beats boardroom language
- Do not sound slick or overly polished
- The goal is to lower the reader's defenses
- Natural humor can help if it feels human, not forced

## Open Curiosity
Good copy opens a loop and keeps it open long enough to earn the click.
- `how to` is strong because it promises useful instruction
- Naming the thing makes it feel real
- `secret` works because people want inside information
- If the reader knows the answer is coming, they keep reading

## Make The Benefit Visible
A reader should be able to picture the product working.
- Use short verbs
- Use concrete nouns and exact details
- Show the result in motion, not just the product sitting there
- Specific imagery is more persuasive than generic description
- Visual cues like bolding and color can highlight key benefits on screen, making them impossible to miss

## Sell Wants, Then Support With Logic
People act on emotion and then look for a reason.
- Start with the want that already exists
- Support the claim with logic, proof, and mechanism
- Use `language of logic` when you need a bigger promise to feel grounded
- Do not sell needs in the abstract if the market is not already hungry for them

## Words That Work
Every word has to earn its keep.
- Short words beat long ones when both say the same thing
- `instantly` implies speed and ease
- A named mechanism is easier to remember than a vague explanation
- Specific language is stronger than hype because it feels earned

## Show the Transformation
People don't buy products. They buy the change they create.
- Use clear before-and-after framing to show real impact
- Demonstrate results in context, not just in isolation
- For example: showing the difference between damaged hair and healthy, full-looking hair without the cost or hassle
- This builds trust faster than any claim alone

## Visual Cues in Copy
Text isn't just words. It's a visual signal.
- Use bolding to emphasize key benefits or outcomes
- Add color (like pink) strategically to draw attention to critical points
- Keep formatting clean and blocky so it stands out on screen, especially in fast-scrolling environments
