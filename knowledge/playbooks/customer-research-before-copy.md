# Customer Research Before Copy

You can't write copy that converts without first understanding your customer. Research is the foundation of every winning message.

## Why This Is Non-Negotiable

You can't outsource this to AI. Reading a summary and calling it done doesn't work. You have to listen. The insight that unlocks your best angle is often hidden in something small, like a complaint about weight or a comment on style. Never delete anything from your research. You don't know what will matter until you've seen the full picture.

The richest data is already in your ecosystem: ad comments, email replies, reviews, and organic posts. These are people who already raised their hand. They're not hypotheticals. They're real customers with real problems.

## The Four-Question Framework

Before writing a single line of copy, answer these four questions:

### Question 1: What's usually true about people buying this?

Build a specific profile. Not "people interested in skincare." Try:
- "Women in their late 30s who've had kids and are struggling with thinning hair"
- "Men with plantar fasciitis who work standing all day"
- "People with bunions who need room for orthotics"

These come from real conversations, not assumptions.

### Question 2: What's their day actually like?

Paint the real picture. Not aspirational. Not polished.
- "Waking up with sore feet, knowing it'll be another long day standing."
- "Trying to find a pair of shoes that don't make your bunions scream."
- "Feeling self-conscious about how your hair looks in a ponytail."

This is where you find the emotional state behind the problem.

### Question 3: Write six benefit bullets

Translate lifestyle into what they get. Not features.
- Weak: "Contains retinol and hyaluronic acid"
- Strong: "Softer, brighter, firmer skin in three weeks"

Ask: What becomes easier? What do they avoid? What do they feel?

### Question 4: Write a Facebook ad

Now write the ad. Hook, what it does (not how), emotional outcome, clear CTA.
Example: "The ramen replacement you knew you needed but struggled to find is finally here. The same quick and easy meal with 10x the flavor. You don't want to miss this weeknight delicacy. Tap shop now before it's gone."

## Where to Get the Data

**Conversations beat surveys.** A survey asks for answers you already think matter. A real conversation lets the customer take you somewhere unexpected. The unscripted stuff is gold.

**Your own brand data is the best source.** Ad comments, email replies, review threads. These are people who care enough to engage.

**Competitor reviews reveal gaps.** Read 20 to 30 reviews of top products in your space. Find complaints. Workarounds. What they're not solving.
- "Felt like a cheese grater" → Your product: gentle and effective
- "Can't find my color match" → Your product: free color replacements

**Amazon cross-purchase data helps with upsells.** See what buyers of similar products are also buying. That tells you what else they're trying to solve.

**Customer testimonials are signal, not decoration.** When multiple people say the same thing, that's your headline. Use their words.

## Real Customer Signals By Category

### Musabella (Orthopedic footwear)

- Core conditions: bunions, plantar fasciitis, neuropathy, heel spurs, flat feet. This is medical need, not preference.
- Wide toe box and room for orthotics are non-negotiable. Not a feature. A requirement.
- Style is the emotional objection. Many customers say the shoes look like "an old lady shoe" or make their feet look fat. They want comfort *and* something they'd actually wear to work.
- Versatility matters: work, travel, daily activity. Color availability (especially all-black) is a real barrier.
- Slip resistance and durability are concerns raised across multiple queries.
- Customers are actively comparing Musabella to other orthopedic shoes, highlighting the need for better arch support and wider toe boxes.

### The Beauty Brand (the hero product)

- Ethical sourcing was a key purchase driver. One customer rejected salon extensions after learning about exploitation and felt better with synthetic.
- Core use cases: thin, fine, or post-pregnancy hair. Volume after weight loss. Ponytail results.
- Videos and reviews were decisive in building trust.
- Natural color matching and low shine reduced hesitation.
- Comfort is a real issue. After four hours, the weight starts to bother people. This is common across the hero product products and should be addressed directly.
- Customers are concerned about ponytail attachment, clips vs band, and how long synthetic hair holds curl.

### Other Products

- The Curvoilá Slimming Shorts' copy leaned into confidence and natural curves, validated by testimonials like: "We guarantee you will never have felt so confident and outgoing than when wearing our shorts."
- The Glitter Lip Kit's success came from long-lasting wear and standout looks, with customers praising performance.
- The GlowUp massager's landing page highlighted acne reduction and improved texture, backed by real results in just weeks.
- the beauty brand Brush campaign showed that customer language around cleanliness became a powerful hook: "I don't have to worry about any of that!"
- The Rainbow Sun Catcher's success was driven by emotional resonance. Customers called it beautiful, well-made, and worth reviewing.
- The Space Saver Drive™, Bite Sucker™, and Vein Exploder™ all had clear use cases backed by testimonials, proving research-driven positioning works.

## The Testing Connection

When your customer profile is sharp, your first round of copy tests isn't guessing. It's testing angles you already know matter. If it doesn't convert, the issue is likely the profile, not the copy.

## The Process

1. Talk to 3 to 5 real customers or prospects.
2. Ask about their day and what frustrated them before they found your product.
3. Write down the patterns.
4. Answer the four questions.
5. Draft benefit bullets and ad copy.
6. Analyze competitor reviews for gaps.
7. Position against what others failed to solve.
8. Test it. If it fails, your profile was wrong.

Takes 30 to 60 minutes. Saves you from writing into the void.
