# Ecommerce Landing Page Structure

A proven template for converting visitors into customers. Used across 30+ product tests with consistent structure.

## The Stack

Every winning landing page follows this exact sequence:

### Section 1: Trust Signals
Leads with three-four trust markers:
- Secure Transaction
- Satisfaction Guarantee (or specific: "30 Day")
- Free Shipping
- Free Returns

These go at the top before any sales copy. They neutralize purchase anxiety before you've even made your pitch.

### Section 2: Attention Headline + Hook
One sentence that combines problem identification with the solution.

Examples:
- "Never carry around a dead Apple Watch again" (Chargla)
- "Freeze your headache away in seconds" (Ther-ICE)
- "Get powerful pigments packed in the cutest little wine bottles" (Wine Lip Tint)
- "Effortlessly remove any nail polish in minutes" (Serona)
- "Detangle the toughest knots in seconds without any damage to your hair" (Electric Hair Detangler)

Followed by 2-3 sentences that agitate the problem and reframe the solution as obvious.

Example from Electric Hair Detangler:
"Detangling hair has got to be the worst thing we have to go through, period. It hurts, it takes ages, it damages our hair. And when we've got a stubborn knot... Oh boy don't even get us started."

Then: "At [Brand] we decided to solve this age old problem once and for all."

### Section 3: Benefit Bullets
Not features. Translate every feature into what the customer gets.

Wrong: "Contains microvibrations that penetrate the skin"
Right: "Deep clean that's impossible with your hands", the same [[specificity-and-exactness]] rule.

Use checkmarks. Keep to 4-5 bullets. Each bullet = one benefit, not multiple.

Examples from tests:
- "Get soft, smooth skin for 6 weeks straight" (not "removes hair")
- "Never worry about painful detangling again" (not "rotating teeth technology")
- "Feel like an expert foodie" (not "high-quality olive oil")
- "Save thousands of dollars over your lifetime" (not "replaces waxing/shaving")

### Section 4: Product In Action
GIF or video showing the product solving the problem, not just existing.

Not: Product sitting on a table
Yes: Person using it, problem being solved in real-time

Examples:
- The beauty brand Brush: Button press, hair comes out in 1 second
- Detangler: Rotating teeth working through knot, no fallout
- Nail Remover: Hand in steam chamber, polish dissolving off
- Ther-ICE: On head, pain visibly relieving

### Section 5: Customer Testimonials
Must follow this format:
- Short quote (1-2 sentences max)
- Specific outcome, not generic praise
- Name + initials + 5 stars

Example from Wine Lip Tint:
"I had a ton of compliments on it and I loved the looks even if it's not for your typical everyday wear", Customer A. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Worst practice: "This product is amazing and I love it", fake testimonials are obvious and kill trust, the exact trap [[testimonial-copy-structure]] warns against.

Winning testimonials:
- "I couldn't believe how painless it was. Usually I dread waxing. This took 10 minutes and no pain." the founder K. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- "My neck pain went away by day 2. I've spent $1000s on chiropractors. This changed everything." the client R. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- "I was skeptical but after one use my nails came out perfect. No damage at all." Customer B. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Section 6: How-To (if applicable)
Step-by-step guide. Only include if the product requires explanation. Otherwise skip.

Example (Serona nail remover):
1. Fill with nail polish remover
2. Turn on, wait for green light
3. Soak nails for 5-10 minutes
4. Polish wipes off easily

### Section 7: FAQ
Answer the objections that stopped people from buying:
- "How long does it last?"
- "Will it work on [specific use case]?"
- "What comes in the box?"
- "Is it safe/comfortable?"
- "How does it work?"
- "Can I use it on [variant]?"

From the wellness gadget:
Q: Will it squeeze my neck?
A: No, absolutely not. The unique triple layer system feature included in the wellness gadget™ expands lengthwise; it only provides a vertical stretching action for your neck.

From Serona:
Q: Can it be used to gently remove press on nails with nail glue?
A: Yes! It will work great for that. Make sure you only keep your nails in for 6 minutes and not the full 15 though.

### Section 8: Social Proof Closer
End with: "Loved by X happy customers, [join/order] yours today"

Variations:
- "Loved by 10,000+ happy customers, join them!"
- "Loved by 354,349 satisfied users"
- "Trusted by thousands"

## Why Each Section Works

Trust signals neutralize risk before selling starts. Headline captures attention. Benefits explain why they should care. Product demo proves it works. Testimonials show real people benefiting. FAQ handles remaining objections. Social proof says others already decided.

This systematic removal of doubt is what converts.

## Testing The Structure

When testing fails, the structure is usually fine. The problem is usually:
- Headline isn't compelling enough (vague benefit)
- Testimonials sound fake (too polished, no specifics)
- Product demo GIF doesn't show the problem being solved
- FAQ doesn't address the real objection
- Trust signals missing or buried

Don't redesign the whole page. Fix the weakest section.

## Section Order Matters

From testing across 30+ products, this order consistently outperforms other arrangements:
1. Trust signals first (removes objection before it forms)
2. Headline next (capture attention)
3. Product in action (prove it works, emotionally)
4. Benefits (why you should care)
5. How it works (satisfy curiosity)
6. Testimonials (social proof)
7. FAQ (handle last objections)
8. Social proof closer (final push)

Changing this order (e.g., benefits before headline, FAQ before benefits) consistently underperformed.

## The Margin Question

Pages that work have a built-in scarcity or offer component:
- "Get 4 for just $29.95"
- "Buy 2 get 1 free"
- "50% off this weekend only"

Pages that struggle often have thin margins reflected in the offer. If your product cost is $8 and you're selling for $9.95, no landing page will convert enough to be profitable.

## Mobile Above The Fold

- Treat mobile above-the-fold as its own layout problem, not a minor desktop tweak.
- Keep the core headline, copy, and image, but move the offer banner or resize elements so the whole value proposition fits without scrolling.
- Test the rearrangement on mobile first even if desktop already looks acceptable.
- Source: ATV Test 3

## Complex Catalogs Need Navigation

- For complex products with many variants, a more navigable collection-style or multi-step page can outperform an oversimplified single-page flow.
- In the hero product test, simplifying the page and removing the direct navigation underperformed the version that let people browse and jump into the right product.
- Pricing tests should move in smaller steps; jumping from `44.95` to `99.95` lost money, while the next test target was `74.95`.
