# Email Subject Lines and Body Copy Formulas

Email is one of the highest-ROI channels for direct response, but most founders treat subject lines as an afterthought. The beauty brand's testing showed specific patterns that work repeatedly.

## The Subject Line Principles

### 1. Curiosity Gap

Create a knowledge gap the reader wants to close.

**Works:**
- "What solar companies don't want you to know"
- "The one thing your dermatologist won't tell you"
- "Why everyone's switching away from [competitor]"

**Pattern:** "The [surprising fact/secret/reason] [audience cares about]"

### 2. Specific Outcome

Instead of vague benefits, state concrete results.

**Works:**
- "Get salon-quality blowouts in under an hour"
- "Remove dead skin in one minute"
- "Results in 72 hours, tested on 1,000 users"

**Pattern:** "[Action verb] [specific result] in [timeframe]"

### 3. Problem Identification

Mirror the reader's existing problem, making them recognize themselves.

**Works:**
- "That moment when you realize your brush is full of dead hair"
- "Hemorrhoids don't get better on their own"
- "You're tired of paying $500/month in electricity"

**Pattern:** "[Specific problem moment/state]"

### 4. Authority/Social Proof

Borrow credibility from others.

**Works:**
- "9 out of 10 dermatologists recommend"
- "Trusted by 50,000+ customers"
- "As seen on [trusted source]"

**Pattern:** "[Number/percentage] of [authority] [endorsement]"

### 5. Exclusivity/Access

Make the reader feel they're part of something special.

**Works:**
- "Exclusive offer inside"
- "Members-only pricing"
- "Limited to first 100 orders"

**Pattern:** "[Exclusive marker] [offer/access]"

## Email Subject Line Testing From the beauty brand

The beauty brand tested multiple subject lines for a Buy 1 Get 1 Free offer. Winners:

**High-performing patterns:**
- "Limited Time Offer: Buy 1 the hero product, Get A 2nd FREE"
- "Breaking News: The Viral hero product is B1G1 FREE Today"
- "Ditch the salon: Buy 1 the hero product and get a 2nd Free Today"
- "The Hair You've Always Wanted Is B1G1 FREE Today"

**What worked:**
- Urgency markers ("Limited Time", "Breaking News", "Today")
- Outcome focus ("The Hair You've Always Wanted")
- Specificity ("the hero product", "B1G1", "2nd")
- Lowercase mixed case for emphasis ("FREE", "B1G1")

**What underperformed:**
- Generic benefits ("Save money", "Amazing results")
- Feature-focused ("Our product has X feature")
- Question format (unless creating curiosity gap)
- All caps (reads like spam)

## Email Body Copy Patterns

### Pattern 1: Problem-Acknowledge-Solution

Start by showing you understand their exact problem, then present your solution as natural next step.

**Example from the beauty brand:**
```
Color matching online is hard😖 That's why we let you get 2 hero products for the price of one, and replace them until they blend so seamlessly your husband will think you grew hair overnight!
```

**Structure:**
1. Name the specific problem (not generic)
2. Show why it's hard
3. Present your solution
4. State the benefit in emotional terms

### Pattern 2: Proof-First

Lead with social proof, then explain the offer.

**Example from the beauty brand:**
```
The Viral hero product is now buy 1 get 1 FREE! 

✅ Instant Confidence Boost
✅ Color Match Guaranteed 
✅ Zero Damage Extensions 
✅ Secure No Slip Fit 

Tap "Shop Now" to get yours while stock lasts👇
```

**Structure:**
1. Hook with the offer
2. Stack 3-4 specific benefits
3. Social proof ("stock lasts", "while supplies last")
4. Clear CTA

### Pattern 3: Scarcity + Benefit

Combine time/inventory scarcity with emotional benefit.

**Example from the beauty brand:**
```
There's only thing better than instantly longer & fuller hair… Getting it for FREE! That's right, if you order your hero product today we'll send you a second one for $0.00😍
```

**Structure:**
1. Emotional benefit statement
2. Contrast with scarcity
3. Specific offer terms
4. Urgency timeline

## Partnership/Affiliate Email Copy

When reaching out to partners for affiliate or co-marketing deals, the email structure matters.

**Pattern: Mutual Benefit Frame**

```
We have a customer base of [X] [avatar] who [specific problem]. 

Our goal is to share [your solution] that helps our customers [desired outcome].

Our campaign plan:
- Email marketing to [segment] with [strategy]
- Social media posts with [approach]
- [Additional channel] with [tactic]

Between these marketing strategies we are sure to have explosive impact on [partner's] sales and create a long-lasting, mutually beneficial relationship.
```

**What works:**
- Specific customer avatar (not "anyone interested in X")
- Problem identification (shows you understand their world)
- Concrete marketing channels
- Emphasis on mutual benefit (not "we want free product")
- Proof of reach ("54,000 followers", "63 million impressions")

## Reminder Email Structure

For incomplete actions like video asks, follow-up emails should be gentle and action-oriented.

**Key principles:**
- Acknowledge progress without judgment: "Hey, we saw that you started the video ask..."
- Include a clear call-to-action with a direct link: "Include the link to the video ask so you can share and embed."
- Keep tone encouraging: Frame completion as a small step toward a free reward.
- Example phrasing: "You're almost there. Tap below to finish your video and get your free product."

## Subject Line Variations for Product Promotions

The beauty brand tested subject lines for Divi products, showing that emotional hooks with urgency and emojis can boost engagement.

**Examples:**
- From My Routine to Yours: Why DIVI Serum is My Go-To for Fuller Hair 💁♀️
- The beauty brand Endorsed✅ Why DIVI's Serum Is The Only One We Trust. Tap to find out!

**What worked:**
- Personal endorsement cues ("the beauty brand Endorsed")
- Emojis that reflect the benefit (💁♀️ for beauty/self-care)
- Urgency or exclusivity markers ("Tap to find out!")
- Specificity in audience reference ("From My Routine to Yours")

## Subject Line Testing Framework

When testing subject lines, bucket them by type:

**Curiosity (CUR):** Create knowledge gap
- "What solar companies don't want you to know"
- "The secret to feeling confident in 60 seconds"

**Problem-Specific (PROB):** Name exact problem
- "Tired of shaving every day?"
- "Hemorrhoids preventing you from living normally?"

**Benefit-Specific (BENEFIT):** State exact outcome
- "Results in 72 hours without pain"
- "Get salon blowouts in 1 hour"

**Urgency (URG):** Time or scarcity
- "Limited Time Offer"
- "Only 50 left in stock"
- "Ends at midnight tonight"

**Social Proof (PROOF):** Authority or numbers
- "9 out of 10 dermatologists recommend"
- "Trusted by 50,000+ customers"

Test 3-5 different types simultaneously. Judge by opens AND conversions (not just open rate).

## Common Email Copy Mistakes

### Weak: "We're so excited to announce..."
Strong: "Color matching online is hard. Here's how we fixed it."

### Weak: "Amazing results await"
Strong: "Results in 72 hours, tested on 1,000 users"

### Weak: "Our product is the best"
Strong: "9 out of 10 dermatologists recommend our formula"

### Weak: "Limited time"
Strong: "Ends tonight at midnight"

### Weak: "We think you'll love this"
Strong: "Here's exactly why this works [proof/mechanism]"
