# Facebook CBO and Creative Volume
A rule for scaling paid social without micromanaging the machine.

## The Core Rule
Never turn ads off just because one creative looks expensive. Low CPA ads often depend on higher CPA prospecting ads feeding the funnel. If you kill the top, the bottom starves.

The bigger rule is this: Facebook compounds. TikTok finds proof, Facebook gives it room to run.

## How To Run It
Use one CBO per product or market.

Keep targeting broad unless you have a real reason not to. Do not over-steer the algorithm.

Load the campaign with enough creatives to let the system sort signal from noise.

For new store tests, think in three-day blocks. Give the campaign enough budget to learn, then stop and inspect the result.

If the test is wildly unprofitable, kill it. If it is close, make a meaningful change in the offer, price, or angle and test again.

If you have a winning creative from TikTok, move it into Facebook and let it breathe.

## Budget and Pacing
Use a budget you can afford to lose for the learning window.

For new tests, the operator's pattern was roughly $50 to $100 a day, or whatever made three days of data meaningful.

Do not make more than two meaningful changes a week.

Do not increase budget more than 20 percent a day unless you are very sure of what you are doing.

## What To Watch
Do not read CPA in isolation.

Watch hook rate, frequency, CTR, and overall campaign profitability.

A higher CPA ad can still be useful if it is feeding the funnel. A lower CPA ad with rising frequency can be a warning sign, not a victory lap.

## Rule-Based Scaling
the beauty brand optimization log added a tighter operating rule.

A simple version:
- Scale up when CPA stays below 17.50 for 3 days
- Scale down when CPA stays above 25 for 3 days
- Only let the automation fire on campaigns that explicitly say scale up or scale down in the name
- Keep the move small, like 20 percent up or down

That naming rule matters. Without it, automation can fire on the wrong campaign and create fake confidence.

The other lesson is that simplification usually beats clever structure. A control campaign with the best post IDs and a clean shop placement pulled a 15 CPA and kept dropping. The proving ground ad sets often got no spend at all. More moving parts did not equal more learning.

One more pattern: scale down by 20 percent can be the right move. In the log, CPA dropped back to 15 or 16 and profit almost tripled after the cut. Sometimes less spend is the fastest path to better margin.

## The Creative Loop
Volume creates learning, the same saturation clock [[market-saturation-and-creative-fatigue]] tracks.

Keep launching new creatives. Study what is working. Talk to customers. Pull better comments, the same loop [[finding-winning-copy-through-testing]] runs. Use the best signals to make the next creative stronger.

When the account gets a winner, do not get timid. Feed it.
