# Offer Framing: How to Present Price So People Say Yes

Same product, same price, different framing = different conversion rates.

## Bundle vs Single Pricing

**Bundle (proven winner):**
- "Get 3 for $29.95" > "Get 1 for $9.95"
- "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" > "33% off"
- Why: Psychology. Bulk feels like a steal. Single feels risky.

Examples from tests:
- Wine Lip Tint: "6 bottles" frame
- Chameleon Shorts: "Buy 2 get 1 free"
- Hearties glasses: "Buy 2 get 1 free"
- Deep exfoliation glove: "Get 3 for $29.95"

**When bundle fails:**
If margins are thin or product is expensive, bundling prices people out. Test single with discount instead.

## Price Anchoring

Compare to alternative cost to make your price feel small.

**Works:** "Salon appointment costs $100, get lifetime access for $39.95"
**Works:** "Costs 3x more at retail"
**Doesn't work:** "Great value" (no anchor), the exact mechanic [[price-anchoring-and-comparison-copy]] details

## Stacked Incentives

Multiple trust signals layered:
- Secure Transaction
- 30 Day Guarantee
- Free Shipping
- Free Returns

Each removes one reason to hesitate. Stack all four at top of page.

## Price Point Psychology

**$29.95 vs $30**
Left-digit dominance: $29.95 reads as "20s" not "30s" to the brain.

Borrowing from the operator's tests:
- Wine Lip Tint: $29.95 (not $30)
- Lash Lift: $50 broken into "get 6 bottles for..."
- Hearties: not mentioned as full price, emphasized as "buy 2 get 1 free"

## Urgency Framing

Works:
- "50% off sale ends today"
- "Limited stock" (if true)
- "[X] already sold today"

Doesn't work:
- "New product" (irrelevant to buyer)
- "Special launch price" (who cares)
- False scarcity (reads as manipulative)

Observation from tests: Urgency helps when offer is strong. Weak offer + artificial urgency kills trust.

## The "No Brainer" Test

Before launching, ask: Would I buy this at this price?

If answer is "maybe" or "I have to think about it," the offer needs adjustment:
- Bundle more units
- Lower price
- Add guarantee
- Anchor against higher alternative

The operator's logs show offers that passed the no-brainer test converted, the same bar [[grand-slam-offer]] sets. Offers that didn't, didn't.

## Testing Price Sensitivity

Midday price drop was tested in some logs:
- Product starts at $X
- By afternoon, drop to $X-5
- Monitor if this resurrects sales

Result: Inconsistent. Usually a sign the product isn't the right fit, not a pricing issue.

## Margin vs Conversion Tradeoff

Higher price = fewer conversions but better profit per unit.
Lower price = more conversions but worse margin.

Observation: the operator tested this. Winners had both decent margins AND compelling offers.

Example: If product costs $8, sells for $29.95 = $21.95 margin. Bundle 3 at $29.95 total = $7.98 margin per unit but 3x volume. Second is better if ROAS >1.5.

## Evidence

2021-11 Wine Lip Tint - Bundle pricing success
2022-05 Chameleon Shorts - Buy 2 get 1 free
2022-05 Hearties - Buy 2 get 1 free
2021-12 Deep Exfoliation Glove - 3 for $29.95
Multiple tests showing price drop midday had minimal impact

## Post-purchase VIP framing

- Send post-purchase email and SMS traffic to an exclusive VIP collection page rather than a generic upsell.
- Frame the offer as a thank-you gift with time-limited access, a visible timer, and a clear discount on secondary products.
- Keep the page congruent with the original purchase so the upsell feels like a natural next step, not a random add-on.
- Use strong compare-at pricing and aim for roughly 60 to 70 percent off on secondary items when the margin allows it.
