# Personality and Authenticity in Copy

Forget polish. Forget trying to sound professional. The most effective copy sounds like a real person talking.

## The Pattern

Across dozens of product tests, the consistently winning copy pattern wasn't about superior structure or clever wordplay. It was about personality.

When copy had voice-when it was funny, specific, self-aware, or authentic-it converted. When it was polished and corporate-sounding, it lost.

## Why It Works

People are trained to ignore salespeople. Corporate copy activates that skepticism, the exact trap [[claude-hopkins-principles]] warns against.

Authentic, personality-filled copy disarms skepticism. It sounds like a friend, not a marketer.

Specific details and humor also make copy memorable. "DUMB GLASSES WORK" sticks. "Results in 72 hours" does not.

## Examples

### ChopSabers (Won Days 1-5)

Landing page headline and opening:

"Turn ordinary ramen or sushi nights into an epic quest in a galaxy far, far away!"

"Feel like meals are getting a little bit boring and repetitive? That means it's time to spice things up with the ChopSabers."

Playful, fun, personality-driven. Not corporate. It worked.

### VertiFree Glasses

Testimonial:

"Swallow your pride and buy these dumb, derranged Minion glasses because they work better than anything I've ever tried. THESE DUMB GLASSES WORK."

Authentic. Admits the product looks stupid. But passionate about it. That honesty is more convincing than corporate praise.

### Bug Snatcher

Testimonial:

"I have been trying to squeeze venom out of my own skin FOR YEARS, thinking somehow that was going to make a difference. Enter the Bug Bite Thing. It sucks the poison out and the bite is gone IN MINUTES."

Personal story. Specific. Real voice. Not generic.

## How to Write with Personality

### 1. Write Like You Talk

Conversational, not formal.

Weak: "This product has been engineered to remove unwanted visible hair"
Strong: "Never shave again"

Weak: "Our innovative solution provides superior facial rejuvenation"
Strong: "Get your face snatched without spending $5,000 on procedures"

### 2. Be Specific and Opinionated

Weak: "Amazing results"
Strong: "Results in 72 hours, then you'll never want to go back"

Weak: "Our product is popular"
Strong: "9 out of 10 dermatologists recommend our formula"

### 3. Admit Flaws

"These glasses look dumb" makes people believe the rest. It shows you're not hiding anything.

Weak: "Beautiful, elegant eyewear"
Strong: "Yeah, they look goofy. But they work."

### 4. Use Natural Humor

When it's natural, humor wins. When it's forced, it kills.

Natural humor acknowledges the oddness or contradiction. VertiFree asks "who doesn't love a good laugh" when wearing goofy-looking glasses.

Forced humor: punchlines that have nothing to do with the benefit.

### 5. Testimonials Need Real Voice

Weak: "This product is amazing and I love it"
Strong: "I've had migraines 3x per week. After two weeks using this, I'm down to one. Finally have my life back." - the founder M.

The strong one has:
- Specific outcome (3x to 1x per week)
- Personal voice
- Named person
- Before/after transformation

## Platform Dependency

Personality works everywhere, but it's essential on:

**TikTok/Instagram:** Audience expects authenticity. Rejects polish. UGC beats professional production.

**Email/Direct:** Conversational tone resonates. Readers feel spoken to, not marketed at.

**Testimonials:** Real-sounding testimonials convert. Fake-sounding ones kill trust.

**Landing pages:** Authenticity (admitting tradeoffs, showing real use) beats generic benefits.

Where formality is warranted:
- **B2B/Enterprise:** Audience expects professionalism
- **Luxury/Premium:** Positioning might be exclusivity and polish
- **Legal/Compliance:** Constraints force formality

## The Paradox

The most effective copy often seems LESS professional than corporate copy. More casual. More willing to take stands.

But it converts better, the same pattern documented across [[ad-copy-winning-patterns]]. Because professional means getting the job done. And the job is sales, not sounding polished.

## What Kills Personality

Self-judgment lines. Hedging. Corporate speak.

Weak: "I know it sounds crazy, but this product really works"
Strong: "This product works"

Weak: "If you're even slightly interested, you might want to try..."
Strong: "You need this"

Weak: "We're the leading provider of innovative solutions"
Strong: "We solve X problem. Here's how."

## Integration with Other Frameworks

Personality pairs with:
- **Specificity** (personality + specific claims = memorable)
- **Problem-Solution** (personality + problem identification = resonant)
- **UGC Format** (user-generated content is inherently more personality-driven)
- **Authenticity in Testimonials** (specific testimonials feel more real)
