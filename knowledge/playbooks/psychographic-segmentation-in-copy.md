# Psychographic Segmentation in Copy

People don't all buy for the same reasons. Same product solves different problems for different archetypes. Effective copy acknowledges the segment and speaks to their specific motivation.

## What is Psychographic Segmentation

Unlike demographics (age, income, location), psychographics are values, motivations, and personality types.

Demographic: Woman, 45-55, $50k-$100k income
Psychographic: Protector archetype - prioritizes health, safety, family wellbeing, prevention

One demographic can contain multiple psychographic segments. Tailor your copy to the psychographic, not the demographic.

## The Four Core Archetypes

### 1. Protector (Relevance: High | Intensity: High | Action Likelihood: High)

**Motivation:** Safety, health, prevention, responsibility

**Psychographic Profile:**
- Prioritizes stability and security
- Concerned with family/loved ones' wellbeing
- Values products that reduce risk
- Motivated by preventing problems, not just solving them
- Appreciates detailed information and guarantees

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with safety and protection language
- Emphasize prevention: "Avoid salon infections", "Prevent further damage", "Protect your skin"
- Include guarantees and risk-reversal
- Use "protect", "safe", "prevent", "ensure"
- Highlight clinical validation or expert endorsement
- Address lurking fears ("won't damage", "certified safe", "dermatologist tested")

**Example from the footcare brand:**
"This is gentle enough and all you need to keep you smooth. I'd decline using that cheese grater tool. But this right here is gentle enough and works perfectly."

The Protector is saying: "This protects my feet from harsh salon tools."

### 2. Warrior (Relevance: Low | Intensity: Low | Action Likelihood: Low)

**Motivation:** Challenge, dominance, conquest, winning

**Psychographic Profile:**
- Wants to be the best
- Motivated by competition
- Values performance and results
- Loves conquering obstacles
- Action-oriented, impatient

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with competitive advantage
- Use "beat", "crush", "dominate", "win", "outperform"
- Emphasize that this is the "best" or "most effective"
- Create urgency through scarcity or limited access
- Highlight superiority over competitors
- **Note:** For cracked heel products, Warrior motivation is LOW because the problem feels defensive (feet are cracked, not conquerable). This archetype prefers products that help them achieve goals, not fix problems.

### 3. Lover (Relevance: High | Intensity: Medium | Action Likelihood: High)

**Motivation:** Connection, beauty, experience, pleasure

**Psychographic Profile:**
- Values sensory experience
- Motivated by beauty, comfort, pleasure
- Wants to feel good and attractive
- Appreciates luxury and self-care
- Seeks to make others happy

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with sensory language
- Use "soft", "smooth", "beautiful", "luxurious", "silky", "pleasure"
- Emphasize the experience of using it
- Paint emotional outcomes ("feel beautiful", "look amazing")
- Include before/after transformation
- Highlight gift-giving potential

**Example from the footcare brand:**
"My feet feel like a baby butt", "Smooth like butter on a biscuit", "My feet look 20 years younger"

The Lover is motivated by the sensory transformation and how it makes them feel.

### 4. Nurturer (Relevance: Medium | Intensity: Low | Action Likelihood: Low)

**Motivation:** Care, support, helping others, community

**Psychographic Profile:**
- Focused on others' wellbeing
- Motivated by helping and supporting
- Values community and relationships
- Appreciates products that help family/friends
- Slower decision-making, wants to be sure

**Copy Approach:**
- Lead with "help your family", "care for loved ones"
- Use "care", "support", "help", "nurture"
- Emphasize gift-giving to others
- Show how this improves family relationships
- Build trust through testimonials from other caregivers

**Example from the footcare brand:**
"I bought this for my 72-year old mom. She loves it. It's helping her care for her feet when she can't do it herself anymore."

The Nurturer is motivated by the ability to help a parent or loved one.

## Scoring Framework: Relevance × Intensity × Action Likelihood

For any product, score each archetype:

| Archetype | Relevance | Intensity | Action Likelihood | Product Fit | Total | Viable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Protector | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 16/20 | Yes |
| Warrior | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 6/20 | No |
| Lover | 4 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 15/20 | Yes |
| Nurturer | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 10/20 | Maybe |

**Relevance:** How much does this archetype care about the problem?
**Intensity:** How deep is the emotional motivation?
**Action Likelihood:** How likely are they to buy?
**Product Fit:** Does your product actually serve this archetype's needs?

Focus copy on archetypes scoring 14+. Test with 12-13 segments.

## Multi-Segment Strategy

Most products serve 2-3 archetypes. Your job is to create copy variations for each.

**Single Ad ≠ All Archetypes**

One ad can't speak to Protector + Lover + Nurturer simultaneously without becoming generic.

**Better approach:**
- Ad Variation 1 (Protector): Safety, prevention, gentle
- Ad Variation 2 (Lover): Sensory outcome, beauty, transformation
- Ad Variation 3 (Nurturer): Helping family, gift-giving

Test all three. Measure separately. Scale the highest performers.

## Identifying Your Prospect's Archetype

Listen for these language patterns in customer feedback:

**Protector language:**
"Safe", "gentle", "won't damage", "certified", "approved", "prevents", "protects", "trust"

**Warrior language:**
"Best", "beats", "never going back", "superior", "finally works", "outperforms"

**Lover language:**
"Beautiful", "soft", "smooth", "luxurious", "feels amazing", "looks young", "pleasure"

**Nurturer language:**
"For my mom", "helps my family", "gift", "care", "support", "my mom loves it"

The footcare brand reviews contain all four patterns. Extract and organize by archetype.

## The Footcare Brand Case Study: Multi-Archetype Messaging

**Protector Focus:**
"This is gentle on the feet. One thing nail shops do is intentionally harshly scrub feet with a cheese grater. But this right here is gentle enough and won't cause damage."

**Lover Focus:**
"My feet feel like a baby butt. My feet look 20 years younger. Best gift to myself ever."

**Nurturer Focus:**
"I got this for my 72-year-old mom for Christmas. She loves it. It's helping her keep her feet smooth when she can't do it herself."

**Warrior Focus:**
"This works better than any callus remover I've tried. I've spent $1,000s on chiropractors and salons. This changed everything."

Each message leads with a different motivation, uses different language, emphasizes different benefits.

## Conflicts Between Archetypes

Sometimes archetypes conflict.

**Warrior vs. Protector:** Warrior says "beat the competition", Protector says "safe and gentle". For an aggressive skincare product, Warrior wants "strong formula", Protector wants "won't damage skin".

**Solution:** Create separate messaging. Don't try to appeal to both in one ad.

## Revenue Opportunity by Archetype

Not all archetypes are equal revenue drivers.

The same testimonial-arc thinking in [[testimonial-copy-structure]] applies once you know the archetype.

For the footcare brand:
- Protector: High commitment, repeat purchases, loyal, lower churn
- Lover: High initial purchase, gift-giving (multiplier effect), may churn
- Nurturer: Medium purchase, gift-giving, loyal if it helps family
- Warrior: Low relevance to problem, unlikely to convert

Build your strategy around high-revenue archetypes first.

## How to Build Archetype-Specific Copy

1. **Identify your high-scoring archetypes** (14+ on scale)
2. **Research their language** from customer feedback and reviews
3. **Write benefits from their lens** (not from your product perspective)
4. **Create copy variations** - one per archetype
5. **Test simultaneously** - measure which archetype converts best
6. **Scale winners** - double down on highest-revenue segment
7. **Refine** - test deeper variations within winning archetype

## Integration with Other Frameworks

Archetypes work with:
- **[[lead-types-and-awareness]]:** Choose lead type based on archetype's awareness level
- **Benefit Ladder:** Build emotional benefits for each archetype
- **Testimonials:** Use testimonials that speak to each archetype's motivations
- **Landing Page Structure:** Order benefits to match archetype priorities
