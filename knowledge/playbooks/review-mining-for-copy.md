# Review Mining for Copy

## What You Are Looking For
Reviews are raw customer language. The job is to pull the exact words people use when they describe pain, relief, skepticism, surprise, and identity.

## What Repeats Matters
Repetition is a signal.

The repeated language in the source material clusters around:

- Comfort
- Relief
- Less shedding, pulling, or breakage
- Better support
- Roomier fit
- Looking good enough to wear in real life
- Not wanting an ugly orthopedic look
- Feeling like walking on a cloud

Those phrases tell you what the market values enough to say out loud, the raw material [[customer-brain-document]] is built from.

## How To Turn Reviews Into Copy
Use customer language as the raw material for hooks, bullets, objections, and proof.

- Keep the customer's syntax when it sounds natural
- Turn specific pain into the opening line
- Turn repeated relief language into proof
- Turn skepticism into a trust-building section
- Turn appearance objections into comparison copy

If the review says it in a plain, believable way, that is usually better than a polished brand rewrite.

## What To Ignore
Do not mine for everything.

- Do not chase the funniest line if it does not sell
- Do not strip out the specific detail that makes the claim believable
- Do not summarize the customer into generic marketing language too early
- Do not force a market angle that the reviews do not support

The market should tell you what it wants, the same discipline behind [[customer-research-before-copy]]. Copy should make that easier to see, not overwrite it.
