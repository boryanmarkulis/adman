# Short-Form Hook Taxonomy

## The Job
Short-form hooks do one thing: stop the scroll fast enough to earn the next 3 seconds.

If you are stuck, do not invent from scratch. Use ten buckets and force four hooks per bucket. That gives you 40 ideas without wandering.

The point is not to be clever. The point is to produce enough good hooks that the market can tell you which angle bites, the same reusable-library approach the beauty brand short form script bank uses.

## The Buckets
- Big premise. Controversial statements, bold statements, target big limiters, predictions, and trend forecasts. Best for attention, comments, and debate.
- Social collaboration. Collaboration with experts, followers, locals, or strangers, plus public reactions and interaction. Best when another human can create proof or friction.
- Challenge and storytelling. Personal challenges, family challenges, DIY, personal stories, and failure lessons. Best when the content is a journey or experiment.
- Big benefit. Big result with little effort. Best when the promise is speed, savings, or ease.
- Puzzles. Questions, quizzes, paradoxes, and what made the difference style comparisons. Best when the hook should create a knowledge gap.
- Patterns. Odd connections, metaphors, unlikely inspirations, unlikely role models, surprising origins, and cultural or historical framing. Best when you want an abstract point to stick.
- New information. Tips, how-to, mistakes, surprising facts, uncommon solutions, and efficiency hacks. Best when the content teaches something useful fast.
- Insight. Things people do not understand, science behind, expert insights, Q&A or interviews, myth busting, perspective shift, hidden influences, secret history, deceptive practices, and debunking hype. Best when the goal is authority or reframing.
- Comparison. Cheap versus expensive, one product versus another, one city versus another, or two similar people with different outcomes. Best when contrast itself is the hook.
- Odd, entertaining, bizarre. Weird gadgets, strange experiments, and unexpected combinations. Best when novelty is the hook.

## What Makes A Hook Work
The best hooks are specific, fast, and easy to understand in one breath.

If the hook is controversial, keep the claim short.
If the hook is educational, make the promise concrete.
If the hook is a comparison, make the contrast obvious.
If the hook is weird, make the weirdness legible fast.

Do not stuff the hook with extra context. Give the viewer one clean reason to keep watching.

## The Prompt
Use this when you need a lot of hook ideas fast:

```text
Generate 4 short-form hook ideas for each of these buckets:
big premise, social collaboration, challenge and storytelling, big benefit, puzzles, patterns, new information, insight, comparison, and odd or bizarre.

Keep each hook short, specific, and compelling.
Make the promise clear in one second.
Avoid vague language.
```

## Believability Rules For UGC Hooks

- Use first-person discovery language instead of ad-speak.
- Lead with a real situation, a concrete result, or a specific time saving.
- Avoid classic "stop scrolling" language when the content can show the problem instead.
- Keep the claim grounded. Overblown lines make the hook feel fake and get skipped.
- Strong hooks combine relevance, novelty, and a believable voice in one sentence, the same CTA pairing [[short-form-video-captions-and-ctas]] builds on.
