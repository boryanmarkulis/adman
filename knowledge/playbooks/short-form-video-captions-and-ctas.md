# Short-Form Video Captions and CTAs

Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) requires different copywriting than long-form ad copy or landing pages. The caption space is limited. The viewer attention window is 2-3 seconds. The CTA must be crystal clear.

## The Two-Part Structure

Short-form video copy has two components: the video hook and the caption CTA.

**The Video Hook** appears in the first 2 seconds and determines whether someone keeps watching.

**The Caption CTA** appears below the video and tells viewers what to do next.

Both matter. Both must be strong.

## Viral Hooks: The First 2 Seconds

Your hook must accomplish one thing: stop the scroll.

Within 2 seconds, the viewer must recognize a problem, become curious, or see something unexpected.

### Hook Types That Work

#### Problem Identification

Open by showing the problem the rest of the video solves.

**Examples:**
- Chapped lips or skin condition visible in first frame
- Hair that's visibly thinning or damaged
- A relatable frustration (hands on face, confused expression)

**Why it works:** Viewers who have this problem recognize themselves immediately.

#### Curiosity Gap

Create a knowledge gap that makes them want to keep watching.

**Examples:**
- "The one thing your dermatologist won't tell you..."
- "This is why your hair won't grow past shoulder length"
- "Nobody talks about this hair loss cause"

**Why it works:** Curiosity is the second-most powerful human motivator after fear.

#### Surprising Contrast

Show a before-and-after or unexpected transformation in the first frame.

**Examples:**
- Split screen of same person, one side with problem, one side without
- Close-up zoom on a result that seems impossible
- Quick cut showing product application and immediate change

**Why it works:** Contrast stops the brain. The brain stops to process inconsistency.

#### Visual Surprise

Something unexpected happens in the first 2 seconds that isn't what they anticipated.

**Examples:**
- Quick zoom to an unexpected detail
- Sound effect that doesn't match the visual (jarring in good way)
- Person reacting with strong emotion (shock, delight, relief)

**Why it works:** Violating expectations captures attention better than meeting them.

### Hook Structure

The strongest hooks combine two elements:

1. **Visual:** What the viewer sees (the main image or movement)
2. **Text/Audio:** What reinforces it (overlay text or voiceover)

**Example:**
Visual: Person scratching scalp uncomfortably
Text: "Stop losing hair in 72 hours"

The visual creates recognition. The text creates specificity and promise.

### Hook Testing

Once you have a winning video format, test different hooks:

- Same video, different opener
- Same product benefit, different problem angle
- Same topic, different emotional angle (fear vs. hope)

Track which hooks get highest watch-through rate and completion rate.

## Caption Writing for Short-Form Video

The caption is your CTA, your brand voice, and sometimes your hook (if the viewer doesn't watch with sound).

### Length

Ideal caption length: 1-3 sentences. 4-5 maximum.

Short-form viewers scan, they don't read. Make every word count.

### Structure

#### Three-Part Caption

```
[Hook or Problem Statement]
[Benefit or Solution]
[Call to Action]
```

**Example:**
"Lost 50 follicles in the shower yesterday 😢
Started using this and saw new growth in 3 weeks
Link in bio to get yours"

#### Two-Part Caption (Most Common)

```
[Problem + Benefit]
[Call to Action]
```

**Example:**
"Tired of thinning hair? This transformed my hair in 90 days
👇 Link in bio"

### Emoji Use

Emoji adds visual interest and breaks up text. Use strategically.

**Do use:**
- 1-2 emojis maximum per caption
- Emojis that match the emotion (sad face for problem, happy for solution)
- Down arrow pointing to bio (📱 or 👇)
- Hand-pointing emojis to direct attention

**Don't use:**
- Random emoji overload (looks desperate)
- Emojis that don't match the tone
- Emoji instead of punctuation (use proper grammar)

**Example (Good):**
"Stopped losing hair 😍 This changed everything 👇"

**Example (Bad):**
"hair extensions ✨💕🌟💫✨💗💫 buy now!!! 🎉🎉"

### Line Breaks

Use line breaks to make caption scannable:

```
Problem

Solution

CTA
```

Whitespace invites reading. Dense text looks spammy.

### Text Overlay Inside the Video

For video captions overlaid ON the video itself:

- **Font:** Clean, readable (sans-serif like Montserrat)
- **Size:** Large enough to read on mobile (18px minimum)
- **Color:** High contrast (white text on dark, or vice versa)
- **Highlight:** Use brand color to bold key benefit phrases
- **Stroke:** Black outline (69px) makes text readable over any background
- **Animation:** Bounce Out or fade (movement keeps attention)
- **Capitalization:** First letter only (sentence case reads more friendly than ALL CAPS)

**Example:**
`First letter capitalized, Brand Pink for key words, Black stroke for readability`

## CTA Patterns That Work

### 1. The Direct Call

```
Link in bio
Link in comments
Swipe up
Tag someone who needs this
```

**Best for:** Most situations. Direct and clear.

**Example:**
"This changed my life. Link in bio"

### 2. The Engagement CTA

```
Like and follow to see more
Follow for more tips
Don't forget to subscribe
Let me know in comments if this worked for you
Comment your result below
```

**Best for:** Building audience, getting engagement metrics.

**Example:**
"Like and follow to see more hair health tips!"

### 3. The Urgency CTA

```
Stocks running low
Price going up
Sale ends [date]
Don't miss out
Before it's gone
```

**Best for:** Products with real scarcity or limited-time offers.

**Example:**
"Only 50 left. Tap link in bio before they're gone"

### 4. The Question CTA

```
Want to know the secret?
Ready to try this?
Curious how it works?
Want this result?
```

**Best for:** Curiosity-gap videos. Sets up next video in series.

**Example:**
"Want to know why your hair won't grow? Link in bio for the full breakdown"

### 5. The Curiosity CTA

```
Wait for the end
Watch till the end
You won't believe what happens next
The secret is in the comments
```

**Best for:** Multi-part series or educational content.

**Example:**
"The #1 reason your extensions fell out? Watch till the end"

### 6. The Share/Tag CTA

```
Tag someone who needs to see this
Share with your bestie
Tag 3 people who should know this
Show your partner this
```

**Best for:** Relatable content, relationship-based products.

**Example:**
"Tag someone who's been asking you about your hair 😍"

### 7. The Multi-CTA

When you want multiple actions:

```
Like ✓ Follow ✓ Link in bio
Comment "YES" + Follow + Link in bio
```

**Best for:** Building audience while driving traffic.

**Example:**
"Like if you want thicker hair ✓ Follow for more tips ✓ Link in bio to get yours"

## CTA Testing

You can test different CTAs on the same video content:

- Same video, different CTA
- Direct (link in bio) vs. Engagement (like + follow)
- Urgency (sale ending) vs. Curiosity (wait for end)

Track click-through rate (bio link clicks) and engagement rate (likes, follows, comments).

**Finding:** Direct CTAs typically drive more sales. Engagement CTAs typically build audience faster.

## Specific CTA Templates

### For Hair Products

**Curiosity angle:**
"Why salon extensions cost $600 when this costs $50 👇"

**Problem angle:**
"Stop hiding your thinning hair. Link in bio"

**Transformation angle:**
"Hair loss went from 50+ strands a week to almost none. Here's what I did 👇"

**Social proof angle:**
"50,000+ women ditched the salon. Now it's your turn 👇"

### For Educational Content

**Completion angle:**
"Save this for later. You'll need it 👇"

**Action angle:**
"Try this one thing. Results in 72 hours. Link in bio for the full guide"

**Curiosity angle:**
"Dermatologists hate this one simple trick 👇"

### For Lead Generation

**Direct:**
"Get your free solar quote. Link in bio"

**Engagement:**
"Comment 'SOLAR' and we'll send you details"

**Hybrid:**
"Like if you want to cut your electric bill in half 👇 Comment your bill amount"

## Common Mistakes

### Unclear CTA

**Weak:** "Check out my site"
**Strong:** "Solar calculator in bio. Free quote in 60 seconds"

The viewer needs to know exactly what to do and why.

### CTA Doesn't Match Video Content

**Bad:** Video about hair loss, CTA says "Follow for makeup tips"
**Good:** Video about hair loss, CTA says "Link in bio for the hair growth guide"

CTA should feel inevitable given the video content.

### Too Many CTAs

**Bad:** "Like ✓ Follow ✓ Comment ✓ Share ✓ Link in bio ✓ Subscribe ✓ Tap tap tap"
**Good:** "Like and follow for more tips. Link in bio for the full guide"

Too many options paralyze viewers. Pick 2-3 maximum.

### Vague Language

**Weak:** "Click the link"
**Strong:** "Click the link for your free quote"

The viewer needs to know why they're clicking.

## Channel-Specific Variations

### TikTok

TikTok CTAs focus on engagement and following:
- "Follow for more tips"
- "Like if you agree"
- "Comment your result"
- Link clicks less important (algorithm prefers in-platform engagement)

### Instagram Reels

Instagram emphasizes saves and shares:
- "Save this for later"
- "Share with someone who needs this"
- "Tag your bestie"
- "Tag 3 people who should know this"
- Link clicks through bio

### YouTube Shorts

YouTube emphasizes subscription and watch time:
- "Subscribe for more"
- "Watch the full breakdown on the channel"
- "More info in the description"
- Link clicks through description

## The B-Roll Principle

When pulling viral content and repackaging it, the video visuals must show the solution, not just the problem.

**Structure:**
- Seconds 0-2: Problem identification (hook)
- Seconds 2-4: Problem being solved (mechanism)
- Seconds 4-6: Result of solution (outcome)
- Seconds 6+: CTA reinforcement

Each section needs B-roll that matches the narration. Don't just show the problem for 30 seconds and then solve it at the end. Show the progression.

## Voiceover Tone

Even with a visual hook, the voiceover needs to match.

**For educational content:**
- Conversational, not robotic
- Informative, not salesy
- Professional, not breathy
- Specific facts, not vague claims

**Example:**
"Rosemary oil stimulates the scalp and increases blood flow. After 12 weeks, users saw new hair growth in thinning areas."

Not:
"Rosemary oil is amazing for your hair and will change everything!"

## Metric That Matter

**For engagement CTAs:**
- Watch-through rate (how much of video people watch)
- Engagement rate (likes + comments + shares / views)
- Follower growth

**For direct CTAs:**
- Click-through rate (people who click link)
- Conversion rate (people who buy / people who click)
- Cost per customer

Test different CTAs and measure which drive your desired outcome.

## Edit Rhythm

- Keep the cut moving. Avoid dead space, keep clips short, and let the first seconds progress immediately into the demo or payoff instead of lingering on static footage. Source: 4/27 Video Edits, Source: 5/2 Video Ask Edits, Source: 5/3 Ads Notes
- Build the video as a sequence: hook, mechanism or product use, result, then CTA reinforcement. The B-roll should show the solution in motion, not just the problem. Source: 4/27 Video Edits, Source: 5/3 Ads Notes
- Make captions legible and uncluttered. Use a simple background or stronger contrast when needed, and avoid dense on-screen text that fights the viewer's ability to read quickly. Source: 4/27 Video Edits, Source: 5/4 Image Edits
- Use audio shifts, including beat drops, to support the hook when they add energy, but do not rely on sound to rescue a weak edit. Source: 5/2 Video Ask Edits, Source: 4/27 Video Edits

## Simple Caption CTA

- End with a simple product-link CTA in the caption instead of adding a long pitch.
- Mention a real, time-bound discount if one exists.
- A direct `grab yours` style close is enough when the video already did the selling.
- Source: Call To Action 11/13/23

## In-Video CTA Placement

- Use the on-video CTA as a light reinforcement, not a second script. Small overlays like "link in bio" or a short prompt are enough when the video already carries the proof. Source: creator video and CTA SOP
- Keep text readable first: high contrast, simple animation, and no b-roll clutter that forces the viewer to choose between the caption and the message.
- When the hook frame is visually strong, it is okay to omit a separate hook title rather than crowd the screen.

## Hook Execution

- The hook works best when visual, text, and sound effect all reinforce the same idea in the first seconds.
- Keep on-video captions short and scannable, often 1-3 words per line, and use brand color only on the phrases that matter most.
- Use quick zoom in/out, small jumps, and selective sound effects to make the opener feel alive.
- Test animated caption styles and hook variants aggressively, then batch content ahead of time so the team stays ahead of schedule.

## Caption Consistency and Style

Captions must be uniform across all content to maintain brand clarity.

- Use a consistent color scheme: light pink background with black text.
- Apply sentence case only: capitalize the first letter of each word, not all caps.
- Keep visual style identical across every video. This includes font, spacing, stroke, and animation.
- Ensure every caption looks the same so viewers instantly recognize the brand, even in a fast-scrolling feed.

This consistency is non-negotiable. It builds trust, reduces cognitive load, and makes content feel professional.

## Formatting for clarity

Treat captions like visual punctuation. Replace commas and full stops with blank spaces so they don't break the flow. This keeps the viewer's eye moving smoothly across the screen. Also remove numbers and censor any unwanted words to maintain a clean look.

Group related words together so each caption line makes sense on its own. Don't split phrases mid-sentence. It helps the message land faster and feels more natural when read quickly.

## Timing and character limits

Use consistent timing and character counts based on your caption style. For double-line captions, aim for 1.7 seconds per line with a maximum of 17 characters. For single-line captions, use 1.5 seconds with 15 characters. This keeps the text readable without overwhelming the viewer.

## Dynamic effects and presets

Add subtle motion to captions using preset effects like horizontal pop or smooth scale in/out. These don't distract but help draw attention to key moments. Apply them consistently across a batch by selecting all captions at once and dropping the effect on.

## Automation with macros

Use macros to automate repetitive tasks like changing text color, boldness, or applying formatting. Set up a preset workspace first so your macro runs smoothly. Include delays between actions to prevent errors caused by system lag. This ensures buttons are clicked only when windows are ready, avoiding missed steps.

You can copy and paste key sequences into a macro for things like bold switches or color changes. Just update the hex code as needed and reuse the setup across videos. This cuts editing time dramatically and keeps your style consistent.
