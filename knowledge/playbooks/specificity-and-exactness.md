# Specificity and Exactness in Copywriting

General statements disappear from memory. Specific claims stick, convince, and convert.

## The Principle

Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding like water from a duck's back.

When you make a specific claim, the reader knows you've measured it, tested it, documented it. You're stating fact, not sales talk.

## Why It Works

**Vague claims** = obvious selling. Reader assumes you're exaggerating. They ignore it.

**Specific claims** = look like facts. Reader believes them because they read like documentation.

### Example

Weak: "Our coffee is delicious"
Strong: "Scored 92 points by the Specialty Coffee Association"

Weak: "Get fast results"
Strong: "Results in 72 hours, tested on 1,000 users"

Weak: "Most people prefer our brand"
Strong: "9 out of 10 dermatologists recommend our formula"

Weak: "Salon-quality blowouts"
Strong: "Salon-quality blowouts in under one hour"

## The Weight of Specificity

A statement may take the same amount of space whether vague or specific. But specific always multiplies results.

### The Math

Vague: "Tungsten lamp gives more light" - reader skeptical
Specific: "Tungsten lamp gives 3.3x the light of carbon" - reader convinced

Same sentence length. Same space. The specificity multiplies effectiveness.

## What Counts as Specific

### Numbers

- "Loses weight" vs "Loses 12 pounds in 30 days"
- "Softens beard" vs "Softens the beard in one minute"
- "Works fast" vs "Results in 24-48 hours"

### Concrete Descriptions

- "Great hair" vs "Glossy, frizz-free hair that lasts 6 days"
- "Feels amazing" vs "Silky to the touch, lightweight, non-greasy feel"
- "Removes wrinkles" vs "Reduces fine lines and deep wrinkles in 12 weeks"

### Measurable Outcomes

- "Your life improves" vs "Spend 2 hours less per week on admin"
- "Better sleep" vs "Fall asleep 15 minutes faster, sleep 1 hour longer"
- "More energy" vs "65% report sustained energy without afternoon crash"

### Comparative Data

- "Costs less" vs "Costs 40% less than leading competitors"
- "Faster than rivals" vs "3x faster than Brand X in independent testing"
- "Cleaner" vs "Removes 99.9% of bacteria vs 80% for our nearest competitor"

## The Limits of Specificity

### Only Claim What You Can Prove

A specific claim is a bold claim. It invites verification. If you state "Results in 72 hours," make sure your testing shows it.

If you guess, you'll be caught. Vague claims hide ignorance. Specific claims expose it.

### The Risk

Speaking specifically is more vulnerable than speaking vaguely. But it's also infinitely more persuasive to those who believe you.

Choose specificity. People respect it. They trust it.

## How to Test Specificity

When you're writing copy, ask:

1. Can I replace a vague word with a number?
2. Can I replace an adjective with a concrete measurement?
3. Can I replace a claim with a result?
4. Can I compare to a known alternative?

Every yes = stronger copy.

## Common Vague Words to Replace

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| Amazing | Results in 2-4 weeks |
| Great | 4.8 out of 5 stars, 1,000+ reviews |
| Fast | 40% faster than competitors |
| Effective | Works for 87% of users in trials |
| Popular | Trusted by 50,000+ customers |
| Affordable | Costs less than one coffee per day |
| Easy | Takes 5 minutes to set up |
| Powerful | Can handle 10x the load |
| Premium | Made from Italian leather, hand-stitched |

## The Irony

Specific claims actually seem more modest than vague ones. Saying "3.3x the light" sounds more honest than claiming to be "the brightest." Saying "Results in 72 hours" sounds more believable than "amazing results."

Specificity looks conservative while vagueness looks like overselling, the same principle [[ad-copy-winning-patterns]] confirms at scale.

Use this to your advantage.
