# Story-Driven Advertorials

Use this when writing long-form advertorials or blog-style sales content that should feel like journalism or a believable story, not a hard ad, the same native feel [[advertorial-funnel-architecture]] is built on.

## Core Rules
- Lead with the reader's real problem, then move into the product.
- Keep the story believable with concrete details, not sales fluff.
- Avoid overusing exclamation marks or overly punchy transitions that break the tone.
- Make the headline and subheadline specific enough to say something useful, but still fit the story.
- Do research first, then rank the most important pain points, benefits, features, and objections before drafting.
- Preserve flow when you simplify. Too much compression makes the copy sound robotic.

## Writing Pattern
1. Name the problem the reader already feels.
2. Add the specific context that makes the problem relatable.
3. Introduce the mechanism or product as the answer.
4. Explain the payoff in plain language.
5. Close with a natural next step, matched to the reader's awareness per [[lead-types-and-awareness]].

## Subheadlines as a story map

- Keep subheadlines shorter and cut any words that do not move the prospect forward.
- Start by listing the strongest USP, problem, or objection first, then the second, third, and so on, and weave those points into the story.
- Each subheadline should carry a distinct piece of information, not filler or vague hype.
- Write the structure first, then fill in the body and call to action around that sequence.
