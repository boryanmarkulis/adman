# Viral Content Sourcing and Repackaging

## The Rule
You do not need to invent every winning idea. You need to find what already pulled attention and make it fit your brand better.

In short-form, content arbitrage is real. The internet already showed you where attention is flowing. Use that.

## How To Source
Look for:
- Viral clips in your niche
- Educational videos with strong retention
- Blog posts and articles that already frame the idea well
- Creator clips that prove the hook works
- Customer content that can be turned into ads

The best source is usually a piece of content that already did the job. If it got attention once, it can probably do it again in a better format.

The team should search by niche terms, then go into creators, comments, followers, related accounts, and tags. It is a volume game.

## How To Repackage
Once you find a good reference:
- Pull the transcript or core argument
- Rewrite it in your voice
- Add a cleaner hook
- Use B-roll that matches the story
- Add a voiceover if needed
- Keep the edit tight
- Export into the right platform format

Do not waste time asking AI to invent the whole thing from scratch, the same rebuild pattern [[viral-educational-video-remake]] follows. AI is useful for synthesis, not magical originality.

Better workflow:
- Find the thing
- Extract the structure
- Improve the clarity
- Make it yours

## Scaling Rules
For testing, recycled content can work.
For scaling, custom content wins.

Ripped content is a shortcut. It is fine when you are learning. It is not the right long-term system if you want to build a durable brand or avoid getting called out.

As you grow, build your own bank:
- Capture your product in use
- Keep a list of creator contacts
- Keep a list of high-performing formats
- Turn winners into repeatable SOPs
- Train people to source, not just to post

The goal is to replace one-off wins with a machine that can produce fresh assets on demand, the same repeatable system [[short-form-content-production-system]] runs.

## Organize By Concept

- Sort source clips by concept before editing. The practical pattern is a folder per angle or topic, plus an extras bucket for miscellaneous clips.
- Keep a separate `best hooks` folder so the strongest openers are easy to reuse when a format starts working.
- The goal is not just finding clips once. It is building a searchable bank that makes future repackaging faster.
