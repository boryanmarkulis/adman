# The control is the strongest result performer, not the biggest spender

## Rule

The control is the ad with the strongest results on real delivery over the last 3 days, ending yesterday. Read spend, result volume and cost per result together. Spend alone does not select it. The cheapest single result on tiny spend does not select it either. No minimum age or tenure.

Then go one level down. Inside a dynamic-creative ad, take the top-spend image, headline and primary text. Those are what the challenger holds.

## Why

Both single-number rules failed on real accounts.

- **Cost per result alone picks noise.** The top three ads sat 7% apart on 5 to 12 leads each, so the "winner" flipped every morning and the loop iterated on the wrong ad for three rounds.
- **Spend alone follows the platform's funding, not the buyer.** Over one 3-day window, ad X spent 34 for 5 leads (6.77 each) and ad Y spent 44 for 1 lead (44.11). The spend rule picked Y. The operator corrected it: iterate on what converts.

Stopping at the ad level also misleads. Real concentrations: 91 to 99% of an ad's spend on one image, 82% on one primary, 96% on a headline that was not the one visible on the post.

## Apply

- Drop ads with zero results. Drop any ad that another ad beats on both result count and cost per result. From the rest, take the one with the most spend. Then sanity-check small samples and close differences by eye. It is a decision aid, not proof.
- Compare only inside the same campaign, result definition, attribution window and funnel.
- Re-select every run. Never carry yesterday's control, or its format, forward by habit.
- A cheap result on tiny spend is a Track B signal, not the control.
- If nothing spent, stop and say so. If spend produced no results, keep the last verified control or label the choice provisional.
- This picks which creative to iterate. It never moves budget or touches a live ad.
